## COMM 210 SMALL GROUP SPEAKING AND COMMUNICATING (3.00 units)

- Hours: 3 hours per week: (3.00 lecture hours/0.00 lab hours)
- Credit status: Credit - Degree Applicable
- Grading: Letter Grade or P/NP
- Advisories: ENGL C1000

Focuses on effective small group presenting and communication. Explores the foundational knowledge and practice of public speaking for successfully communicating informative and persuasive messages within the small group context. Introduces students to the theories, techniques, and methods of small group communication. Develops speaking, participation, and leadership skills of effective communicators within the small group setting. Provides applied practice through faculty supervised and evaluated oral group presentations delivered to a live audience, through group activities, and through project group work. Involves weekly outside of class team meetings.

Transfer: C-ID:COMM 140

## COMM 212 INTERCULTURAL COMMUNICATION (3.00 units)

- Hours: 3 hours per week: (3.00 lecture hours/0.00 lab hours)
- Credit status: Credit - Degree Applicable
- Grading: Letter Grade or P/NP
- Advisories: Eligibility for ENGL 201A

Focuses on the importance of culture and its effect on communication. Examines interactive patterns of communication across various cultural contexts (international, ethnic, gender, and class) and assess different methods and techniques of adapting communication to enhance and overcome the difficulties of intercultural communication. Analyzes how cultures adapt, borrow, communicate verbally and non-verbally, and are represented in a diverse world.

Transfer: CSU; UC C-ID:COMM 150

## COMM 215 ARGUMENTATION AND DEBATE (3.00 units)

- Hours: 3 hours per week: (3.00 lecture hours/0.00 lab hours)
- Credit status: Credit - Degree Applicable
- Grading: Letter Grade or P/NP
- Prerequisites: ENGL C1000

Focuses on critical thinking skills necessary for effective argumentative writing and oral advocacy and debate. Advances knowledge and skills necessary for building, defending, evaluating, and refuting arguments through written composition and verbal speaking and debating for various audiences and within various rhetorical and social contexts. Includes evaluation and use of evidence, sound reasoning, and credible sources. Involves extensive writing and verbal public communicating. A minimum of 5000 words of writing is required in written student work from a combination of formal and informal writing such as debate case creation and rebuttal, essays, advocacy speech writing, self and peer evaluation, etc. Students will communicate many of their written arguments through wellprepared faculty-supervised and evaluated speeches and debates to a live audience.

Transfer: C-ID:COMM 120; CSU; UC

## COMM 280 FUNDAMENTALS OF HUMAN COMMUNICATION (3.00 units)

- Hours: 3 hours per week: (3.00 lecture hours/0.00 lab hours)
- Credit status: Credit - Degree Applicable
- Grading: Letter Grade or P/NP
- Advisories: ENGL C1000

Introduces students to the history and symbolic process of human communication through the study of basic communication models, theory, and relevant research findings. Emphasis will be placed on achieving an understanding of the communication process, and the process through which researchers in the field of Communication Studies add to their existing body of knowledge.

Transfer: C-ID:COMM 180; CSU; UC

## COMM C1000 INTRODUCTION TO PUBLIC SPEAKING (3.00 units)

- Hours: 3 hours per week: (3.00 lecture hours/0.00 lab hours)
- Credit status: Credit - Degree Applicable
- Grading: Letter Grade or P/NP
- Advisories: ENGL C1000

In this course, students learn and apply foundational rhetorical theories and techniques of public speaking in a multicultural democratic society. Students discover, develop, and critically analyze ideas in public discourse through research, reasoning, organization, composition, delivery to a live audience and evaluation of various types of speeches, including informative and persuasive speeches.

Transfer: CSU; UC C-ID:COMM 110
