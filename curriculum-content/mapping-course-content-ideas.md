# Advanced Mapping and Photogrammetry - Content Ideas

This document captures content ideas for the advanced mapping course as they emerge. Curriculum is still in development. AERO 100 serves as a prerequisite.

## Course Philosophy

The goal is providing students a foundation they can build on, not comprehensive mastery of every tool. Students should leave with practical skills they can apply immediately while understanding where to go deeper.

## RTK and Positioning

### Why RTK Matters

RTK capability is essential for professional mapping accuracy. This represents a significant portion of curriculum content since understanding positioning fundamentals distinguishes professional work from casual mapping.

### Base Station Operations

Cover tripod placement, NTRIP versus local base corrections, and coordinate systems and datums. Students should understand why positioning matters and how different correction methods work.

### Rover Techniques

Survey pole technique, bipod use for stability, and efficient point collection workflows. Hands-on practice is essential.

### Equipment Philosophy

The course uses Emlid equipment. While some employers use Trimble, the fundamental skills transfer between platforms. Adaptability to different tools is part of being an effective drone operator. Emlid offers excellent value, particularly appropriate for students or organizations building programs from scratch.

## Ground Control Points

Ground control points are fundamental to mapping curriculum.

### Placement Strategy

Cover distribution across sites, edge and center coverage, and adjustments for terrain variation. Students should understand why GCP placement matters for final accuracy.

### Marking Techniques

Target types and visibility considerations, permanent versus temporary markers, and practical field workflows.

## Flight Planning

### Terrain Following

Terrain following is critical given San Luis Obispo's variable topography. Cover how terrain following algorithms work and when to use them versus constant altitude missions.

### Overlap and Coverage

Explain why overlap matters for photogrammetry reconstruction, standard overlap percentages for different applications, and tradeoffs between coverage quality and flight time.

### Mission Planning Workflows

Site assessment procedures, airspace considerations, and weather window planning. Students should develop systematic pre-flight planning habits.

## Photogrammetry Processing

### Foundation Skills

Basic processing workflow, conceptual understanding of what the software does, and interpreting results. The goal is competent operators who can produce deliverables.

### Advanced Topics

For students seeking deeper knowledge: ground control point optimization, dense cloud settings, mesh generation options, and orthomosaic quality assessment. These topics can be optional or offered as extended content.

### Software Approaches

Cloud-based processing (DroneDeploy) offers the simplest path to producing maps. Desktop software (Metashape) provides more control and is software students can grow into over time. Open source options (OpenDroneMap) are worth mentioning as alternatives.

## Data Management

### Folder Structure

Every organization develops its own workflow. Teach a baseline structure that works and that students can adapt to future employers' requirements.

### Archival Considerations

What to keep (raw imagery, processed outputs, project files) and organizational principles for long-term project management.

## Potential Specialization Tracks

Per Ron's notes, the program could develop certificate specializations in specific application areas.

### GIS and Survey Focus

Content tailored for engineers and surveyors, integration with GIS software platforms, and deliverable standards for professional survey work.

### Public Safety Applications

Police operations, fire department support, and search and rescue applications. Each has specific operational considerations and deliverable requirements.

### Agricultural Operations

Crop monitoring and spraying applications. Note Part 137 considerations and aerial applicator license requirements if spray operations are included.

## Equipment-Related Content

### Enterprise Drone Operations

Cover Mavic 4T (thermal) and Mavic 4E (mapping) capabilities and applications. Highlight differences from consumer drones in workflow and capabilities.

### Field Operations

Thermal management during extended field operations, battery logistics for longer missions, and crew coordination for professional operations.

## Future Content

These topics require additional investment and curriculum development.

### American-Made Platforms

NDAA compliance requirements and when non-DJI platforms are necessary. Inspired Flight is a local option worth exploring when budget allows.

### LIDAR

When photogrammetry limitations require LIDAR, fundamental LIDAR concepts, and processing workflows. This represents a substantial curriculum expansion requiring dedicated development and significant equipment investment.
