# UAS Certificate Structure, Working Draft

**Status:** working draft for discussion, July 13, 2026. This supersedes the certificate structure in `research/certificate-specialization-proposal.typ` (March 2026) and revises the structure in Ron's draft program approval form. Course numbers for undeveloped courses remain placeholders.

## Overview

The program is built as a trunk. Every student enters through AERO 100, branches into a focused Certificate of Specialization, and can continue to the Certificate of Achievement, which is the program's comprehensive credential and its financial aid vehicle. The design principle is that the two certificate layers serve different goals. Specializations are short, focused, and achievable, finishable in two to three semesters. Certificates of specialization are allowed to contain elective options, and Cuesta's own GIS certificate uses one, but ours stay deliberately on rails because every option in a small certificate is another course that must fill for someone to finish. The Certificate of Achievement is broad and flexible, and that is where the wide elective pool lives, because choice at that layer helps completion rather than hurting it.

The program deliberately borrows existing courses from four other divisions rather than building parallel curriculum. Every borrowed course also counts toward its home department's own credentials, so the certificates create cross-enrollment in both directions. Their students add our drone courses to their pathway, and our students discover their programs. For several tracks we are the value add to an existing pathway, not the main dish, and that is a feature.

AERO 100 prepares students for the FAA Part 107 certificate and builds flight proficiency to NIST Level 2 standards. AERO 110 covers professional mapping from sensor fundamentals through RTK positioning, photogrammetric processing, and ASPRS accuracy validation, and it now absorbs the multispectral and agricultural remote sensing content that was previously planned as a separate AERO 130. AERO 120 covers thermal imaging and infrastructure inspection aligned with Level 1 thermography and the PG&E and IBEW 1245 partnership. AERO 130 covers spray operations and the California regulatory stack of Part 137, the 44807 exemption, and QAC/QAL licensing. Ron is developing the agricultural angle with Aubrey.

AERO 247 is the capstone mechanism. A student contracts with an instructor for a mentored, real-world drone project with a defined deliverable, following the college's existing independent study pattern (written contract, scheduled check-ins, minimum hours at the 54 hour per unit divisor). It gives the program a standing way to put a student on a community problem and gives the student a portfolio project beyond the certificate. It is intentionally one option among the one-unit boosters, not a requirement, so mentor capacity never becomes a completion bottleneck.

### One-unit booster menu

The Certificate of Achievement requires one unit from a menu of one-unit AERO courses. The menu is a container that grows over time, and because it offers choices, no single booster has to run for students to finish. At launch the menu needs only AERO 247, which requires no scheduled section. Candidates to add later include Ron's planned one-unit public safety introductions and a BVLOS and Part 108 operations booster once the regulatory picture and instructor expertise exist.

## Certificates of Specialization

**UAS Mapping (9.0 units).** AERO 100, GEOG/GEOL 230, AERO 110. Targets the largest commercial drone employment sector. Graduates leave with the gateway course of Cuesta's existing GIS Certificate of Achievement already complete, a deliberate stacking pathway toward that credential.

**UAS Inspection (6.0 units).** AERO 100, AERO 120. The fast track for the utility and inspection workforce, built around the PG&E and IBEW relationship. At six units it falls below the eight-unit threshold in the program approval form, which may simplify approval. It can grow to nine units later if the partnership produces a second course, such as thermography certification preparation.

**UAS Crop Monitoring (9.0 units).** AERO 100, AERO 110, AGPS 242. AERO 110 carries the multispectral imaging, vegetation index, and prescription map content, and AGPS 242 provides the plant science needed to interpret what the imagery shows. An earlier draft paired this track with the GIS courses instead, but reaching GEOL 234 requires GEOL 230 first, which pushed the certificate to 12 units of mostly GIS coursework, and AERO 110 already covers the large majority of the remote sensing content this track needs. Students who want the deeper GIS treatment can get it through the Certificate of Achievement pool or Cuesta's GIS certificate.

**UAS Spray Operations (9.0 units).** AERO 100, AGPS 242, AERO 130. The highest revenue segment of agricultural drone work, paired with the plant science grounding to operate responsibly.

**UAS Videography (9.0 units).** AERO 100, ART 249A, FTVE 225B. The two support courses are the two halves of the craft, still composition and video production and editing. They span two divisions, which costs one extra relationship and buys two departments with a stake in the program. Both courses anchor their home programs' own credentials, so scheduling risk is low.

The UAS Public Safety specialization from the draft approval form is dropped. Public safety re-enters later as one-unit boosters, consistent with the plan for that track and with the completion rate risk of courses that may not fill.

## Certificate of Achievement: UAS Technology (16.0 units)

The comprehensive credential and the reason the program clears the 16 unit federal financial aid floor. The recipe is AERO 100 (3 units), two AERO electives from 110, 120, and 130 (6 units), two support courses from the pool above (6 units), and one unit from the booster menu (1 unit).
