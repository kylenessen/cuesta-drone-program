## 2026-07-15 (flight assessment models for the capstone)

- Added [Flight Proficiency Assessment Models](analysis/flight-proficiency-assessment-models.md) in response to Kyle's reflection that the capstone "driver's test" is undecided and that his instinct is to reward careful, competent control rather than time-trial speed or athleticism. The page lays out three assessment families rather than prescribing one: timed self-scoring (the NIST lane), the competence-based checkride, and tolerance-based scoring.
- Core finding that supports the instinct: on a timed NIST lane the Clemson data shows 93% pass image capture but only 48% meet the time, so time is the discriminator and simply dropping the clock would stop the test separating pilots. A control-first capstone needs a precision-based scoring basis, not NIST-minus-the-clock. Two real precedents are documented as the control-focused model: Canada's RPAS flight review (competence-based, untimed, TC-approved reviewer, demonstrates hover, figure eights, orbits, straight-line navigation, simulated motor failure, safe landing; exercises in TP 15263 Appendix A and the Flight Reviewer's Guide) and the UK GVC practical (a CAA certificate of competency, ~2 hours, site survey plus controlled manoeuvres plus emergency drills). Also recorded: NIST leaves the pass benchmark to the administrator, so the cheap lane apparatus can be regraded on precision with a generous time ceiling, and ASTM F3266 as the underlying competency standard.
- Cross-linked the new page from [NIST Flight Proficiency Programs](analysis/nist-flight-proficiency-programs.md), [Basic Proficiency Evaluation for Remote Pilots](exams/basic-proficiency-evaluation-for-remote-pilots.md), and [NIST Open Test Lane](exams/nist-open-test-lane.md) (added the administrator-sets-the-metric point there), and registered it under Analyses in [index.md](index.md).
- Sourcing note: the Canadian maneuver list is corroborated by a training-provider summary pending the primary TP 15263 Appendix A text, the UK practical detail rests on a CAA-approved provider page alongside the CAA's own certificate page, and ASTM F3266 is paywalled so it is described at the level of its published scope. The capstone decision itself is left open.
