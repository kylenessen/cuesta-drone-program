# Remote Pilot Physiology

**Remote Pilot Physiology** covers the human body's failure modes as they affect drone operations: impairment from alcohol, drugs, and medication, degraded judgment from stress and fatigue, environmental stressors like heat and dehydration during field work, and the limits of human vision, especially at night. The remote pilot, visual observer, and anyone manipulating the controls are all safety-critical humans standing in the sun, so this material is field craft as much as test content. [1](/sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md) [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

## Impairing Substances

The regulatory numbers come from the alcohol and drug rules in the [Part 107 Operating Rules](/regulations/part-107-operating-rules.md): no crew duty within 8 hours of consuming alcohol or with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.04 percent or greater. Physiologically, impairment persists beyond both thresholds, and hangover effects alone degrade performance. Medications matter as much as alcohol on the exam: sedating over-the-counter antihistamines are the classic disqualifier, and the study guide's rule of thumb for a newly prescribed drug is to wait at least 48 hours after the first dose to judge side effects before flying. The honest self-assessment framework is IMSAFE (Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion), taught under [Aeronautical Decision-Making](/topics/aeronautical-decision-making.md). [1](/sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md)

## Stress, Fatigue, and Environment

Stress is cumulative and narrows attention; fatigue, whether acute from a short night or chronic from sustained sleep debt, impairs judgment in ways the impaired person underestimates, and the study guide's benchmark remedy is rest, including 8 hours of sound sleep after exertion. Dehydration and heatstroke are the drone-specific environmental hazards, since remote pilots work outdoors, often on pavement in summer, with attention locked on an aircraft rather than on their own condition. Scheduled hydration and shade for the crew belong in the operational plan, which gives the AERO 100 field labs a concrete procedure to model. Hyperventilation, triggered by stress, produces dizziness and tingling that resolve by slowing the breathing rate; the exam expects recognition of its symptoms. [1](/sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md) [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

## Vision and Night

Seeing a small aircraft against the sky is genuinely hard, and the ACS tests the factors affecting vision: scanning in small sectors, the blind spots created by an empty featureless sky, and the physiology of dark adaptation. At night, the eye's rod cells take roughly 30 minutes to adapt fully to darkness, off-center viewing detects dim objects better than staring directly, and bright light exposure resets adaptation. Night illusions, such as autokinesis, where a stared-at single light appears to move, can misplace the aircraft or other traffic in the pilot's perception. These elements were added to the ACS with the 2021 night rules and pair with the equipment side of [Night Operations](/topics/night-operations.md).
