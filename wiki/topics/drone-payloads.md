# Drone Payloads

A **drone payload** is the equipment a professional drone carries to do useful work, as distinct from the airframe that flies it. Far and away the most common payload is a camera, but the value of thinking in payloads is that it opens the door to every other kind of sensor a drone can lift. The airframe-and-payload distinction is introduced in [Uncrewed Aircraft](uncrewed-aircraft.md). [1]

## Cameras as Passive Sensors

A camera is a passive sensor. It measures light that is already present in the environment, reflected from surfaces, without producing any signal of its own. The overwhelming majority of measurements drones make in the world are ordinary color photographs. Most are used simply as pictures, though as [Digital Imaging](digital-imaging.md) and [Multispectral Imaging](multispectral-imaging.md) show, the same images can be used analytically as data. [1]

## Passive versus Active Sensors

The key division among sensors is passive versus active. A passive sensor measures a signal that already exists, as a camera measures reflected sunlight. An active sensor carries its own source of energy, emits it, and measures what returns. LiDAR is the common active example: it transmits laser pulses and times the light scattered back, using the delay to measure range and build up three-dimensional structure. [2] Because the sensor supplies its own illumination, an active instrument can work independent of sunlight, which is one of its defining advantages over a passive camera. Sonar and ground-penetrating radar are also active and are useful for looking below the surface, for instance sensing underground water, work often shown mounted on a helicopter carrying a large instrument. [1]

## Other Payloads

Payloads reach beyond imaging. Environmental sensors measure temperature, wind, and pressure. Under a broad definition, a weather balloon counts as an uncrewed platform, since it is built to carry a payload aloft, take measurements, and report them back. The concrete example is the radiosonde the National Weather Service flies twice a day: a small expendable instrument package slung beneath a balloon that transmits pressure, temperature, humidity, and GPS-derived wind data every second as it rises, exactly the airframe-plus-payload logic that defines a drone applied to a balloon. [3] That framing connects drones to the wider family of measurement platforms in [Remote Sensing Platforms](remote-sensing-platforms.md). Payloads can also be non-sensing, including the weapons of the military drones from which the technology descended, as noted in [Uncrewed Aircraft](uncrewed-aircraft.md). [1]
