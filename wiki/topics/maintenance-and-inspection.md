# Maintenance and Inspection

**Maintenance and Inspection** covers how a remote pilot keeps a small UAS in a condition for safe operation, which is the actual regulatory standard: Part 107 prescribes no inspection program and no certificated mechanic, but section 107.15 requires the aircraft to be in a condition for safe operation and section 107.49 requires a preflight check before every flight. The FAA's guidance fills the gap with manufacturer instructions and recordkeeping practice, and the ACS tests it as the final Operations task. This material corresponds one-to-one with the maintenance unit required in the AERO 100 outline. [1](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

## Maintenance Practice

Maintenance includes scheduled items from the manufacturer's documentation (firmware updates, motor and propeller service intervals) and unscheduled repair after damage or malfunction. Where the manufacturer provides procedures, follow them; where it does not, the FAA expects the operator to develop sensible procedures and to have maintenance performed by a person with the necessary skills, which under Part 107 can be the owner. Replacement parts should match the manufacturer's specifications, and any aircraft whose condition is in doubt stays on the ground until inspected. For Category 2 and 3 [Operations Over People](/regulations/operations-over-people.md) aircraft, the manufacturer's remote pilot operating instructions and maintenance requirements attached to the Declaration of Compliance become mandatory rather than advisory. [1](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

Recordkeeping is not required for standard Part 107 operations, but the AC recommends logging flights, maintenance, and battery cycles because records reveal wear trends, support resale and insurance, and demonstrate diligence after an incident. A program aircraft fleet is exactly the environment where logs pay off, and requiring students to keep aircraft and battery logs models professional practice. [1](/sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## Preflight Inspection

The section 107.49 preflight is both paperwork and hardware: assess the operating environment (weather, airspace, people, obstructions), brief participants, and inspect the aircraft, including control links, sufficient power for the intended flight, attachment security of any payload, and the flight-critical systems the manufacturer lists. The AC's inspection guidance adds checking registration and marking, control station software state, and lost-link settings. Batteries deserve their own inspection habit, as covered under [Emergency Procedures](/topics/emergency-procedures.md): physical condition, charge state, and secure mounting. For flights at night or in civil twilight, the preflight adds verifying the anti-collision lighting works and will keep working for the flight's duration, per [Night Operations](/topics/night-operations.md). [1](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)
