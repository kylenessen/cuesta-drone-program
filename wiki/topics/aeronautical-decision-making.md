# Aeronautical Decision-Making

**Aeronautical Decision-Making** (ADM) is the FAA's term for a systematic approach to consistently determining the best course of action for a flight's circumstances, and it is the conceptual heart of the human factors material on the [Part 107 Knowledge Test](/exams/part-107-knowledge-test.md). The premise, carried over from decades of manned aviation accident analysis, is that most accidents trace to human decision chains rather than mechanical failure, so the FAA tests the pilot's ability to recognize hazards, assess risk, and manage the humans in the operation. This maps to the required Human Factors unit in the AERO 100 outline. [1](/sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md) [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

## Hazardous Attitudes and Risk

The study guide identifies five hazardous attitudes that corrupt decision-making: anti-authority (don't tell me), impulsivity (do it quickly), invulnerability (it won't happen to me), macho (I can do it), and resignation (what's the use). Each has a memorized antidote, such as follow the rules, they are usually right for anti-authority. Exam questions typically present a scenario and ask which attitude it displays, so scenario practice is the right classroom format. [1](/sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md)

Risk assessment formalizes the go or no-go call: identify hazards, assess each by likelihood and severity, and mitigate until residual risk is acceptable. Checklists like PAVE (Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External pressures) and IMSAFE (Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion) structure the preflight self-assessment, with IMSAFE elaborated under [Remote Pilot Physiology](/topics/remote-pilot-physiology.md). External pressure, the client waiting on a deliverable, is the drone-specific hazard worth stressing to career-track students. [1](/sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md)

## Crew Resource Management and Situational Awareness

Crew Resource Management (CRM) applies team communication to the flight: the remote pilot in command briefs the visual observer and any person manipulating the controls on roles, communication methods, contingencies, and the transfer of control before flight, and maintains effective communication throughout, as the visual observer rules in the [Part 107 Operating Rules](/regulations/part-107-operating-rules.md) require. Situational awareness is the accurate perception of everything affecting the operation (aircraft state, airspace, people, weather, battery) and it degrades under task saturation, which is why the ACS lists task management as a knowledge element. Distributing tasks, such as dedicating the pilot's eyes to the aircraft while a crewmember handles bystanders, is the practical CRM lesson for small operations.
