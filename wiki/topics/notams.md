# Notices to Airmen (NOTAMs)

A **Notice to Airmen (NOTAM)** is time-critical aeronautical information that is either temporary or not known far enough in advance to publish on charts or in other operational publications, disseminated immediately through the FAA's NOTAM system. For a remote pilot, NOTAMs are the mechanism by which yesterday's legal flying site becomes today's restricted airspace: temporary flight restrictions arrive as NOTAMs, and AC 107-2A directs the remote pilot in command to check for NOTAMs before each flight as part of the section 107.49 preflight assessment. The [Part 107 Knowledge Test](../exams/part-107-knowledge-test.md) tests the NOTAM system directly, including flight restrictions designated by NOTAM and how to obtain an established NOTAM through Flight Service. [1](../sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md) [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md) [3](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

## What NOTAMs Announce

NOTAMs carry anything essential to flight safety that the permanent publications cannot: hazards such as air shows, parachute jumps, kite flying, and rocket launches; flights by heads of state and other protected persons; inoperable lights on tall obstructions; temporary obstacles near airfields; runway, taxiway, and airport closures; changes in the status of navigational aids; and even the passage of flocks of birds through airspace. Much of this content is written for manned aircraft, but a remote pilot reads the same notices to decide where and when to operate: a crane NOTAM marks an uncharted obstacle, a parachute-jumping NOTAM marks descending traffic, and an airport-closure NOTAM changes the local traffic picture entirely. [1](../sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md)

## Types of NOTAMs

The FAA categorizes NOTAMs by scope and authority. NOTAM (D) information receives distant dissemination beyond the originating area and covers airports, facilities, and en route conditions. Flight Data Center (FDC) NOTAMs are regulatory: amendments to instrument procedures, changes to airways, and, most importantly for drone operations, temporary flight restrictions. The system also carries military NOTAMs for military fields and airspace, international NOTAMs, and center area NOTAMs filed under an Air Route Traffic Control Center when a condition affects many airports at once. [4](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/notam/what_is_a_notam) [1](../sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md)

## Temporary Flight Restrictions

A TFR is designated by an FDC NOTAM that begins with the phrase FLIGHT RESTRICTIONS, followed by the location, effective period, the restricted area defined in statute miles, the altitudes affected, the coordinating FAA facility, and the reason. TFRs protect people and property from hazards, keep airspace clear for disaster relief and firefighting aircraft, prevent congestion over events of public interest, protect the President and other public figures, and support space operations. They bind small unmanned aircraft exactly as they bind manned aircraft, and wildfire and VIP TFRs are the two kinds most likely to appear suddenly over a California operating area. The FAA's TFR site at tfr.faa.gov gives a graphical picture, and [Airspace Classification](airspace-classification.md) places TFRs among the other designated airspace areas. [1](../sources/remote-pilot-study-guide.md) [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## How a Remote Pilot Checks NOTAMs

The professional habit is a NOTAM and TFR check in the same preflight pass as the weather briefing described in [Aviation Weather Sources](aviation-weather-sources.md). The primary channels are the FAA's NOTAM Search portal, a free Flight Service briefing account at 1800wxbrief.com, and the TFR list at tfr.faa.gov. Drone-specific apps repackage the same data: the FAA's B4UFLY experience and the LAANC provider apps described in [Airspace Authorization](airspace-authorization.md) display active TFRs and airspace advisories on a map, which is how most working remote pilots first see them. The caution is that a LAANC authorization does not relieve the NOTAM check, because a TFR can be issued after the authorization was granted and the authorization does not exempt the flight from it. Older FAA materials, including the Remote Pilot Study Guide, point to the retired PilotWeb site; NOTAM Search and 1800wxbrief are the current front doors. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md) [4](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/notam/what_is_a_notam) [5](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/laanc)

## The NOTAM System and Its Modernization

The plain-text, heavily abbreviated NOTAM format and the system behind it date to 1985, and the system's fragility became national news in January 2023 when an outage of the legacy US NOTAM System halted departures nationwide. The FAA has since been replacing it: a cloud-based NOTAM Management Service (NMS) began deployment in late 2024, the first modernization phase retired the oldest legacy interface in 2025, and the FAA expects the full transition, retiring both the US NOTAM System and the Federal NOTAM System, by spring 2026. Students should also know the naming history, since materials in circulation disagree: NOTAM stood for Notice to Airmen from the beginning, was redefined as Notice to Air Missions in December 2021, and was changed back to Notice to Airmen in February 2025. None of this changed what a NOTAM is or how it reads. Access points may keep shifting as the transition completes, so teach the checking habit against the FAA's current NOTAM page rather than any single URL.
