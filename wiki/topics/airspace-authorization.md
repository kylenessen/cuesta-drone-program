# Airspace Authorization

**Airspace Authorization** is the prior permission section 107.41 requires before a small UAS operates in Class B, C, or D airspace or in Class E airspace designated for an airport. In practice nearly all authorizations flow through LAANC, the Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability, an automated data exchange between the FAA and approved private service providers that grants access in near real time. Students should leave the course knowing not just the rule but the workflow, because requesting LAANC authorization is a routine preflight task in commercial work. [1](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/laanc) [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## How LAANC Works

The FAA publishes UAS Facility Maps that divide the controlled airspace around participating airports into grid squares, each with a maximum altitude at which automated authorization is available (for example 100, 200, or 400 feet AGL). A pilot requests authorization through an FAA-approved service supplier's app, the request is checked automatically against the facility map, TFRs, and airspace data, and approval for operations at or below the grid altitude returns in near real time. Requests above the grid value go to Further Coordination, a manual review by the air traffic facility that Part 107 pilots can request up to 90 days in advance. About a dozen approved suppliers offer LAANC apps; the FAA maintains the current list. [1](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/laanc)

Where an airport's airspace is not LAANC-enabled, the pilot instead applies through the FAA DroneZone portal, which is slower and should be planned weeks ahead. Authorization is distinct from a [Certificate of Waiver](/regulations/certificate-of-waiver.md): an authorization permits an otherwise-standard operation in controlled airspace, while a waiver permits deviating from an operating rule. The paperwork students will encounter blurs these because DroneZone handles both. [1](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/laanc) [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md)

An authorization does not relieve any other rule: the operation must still comply with the [Part 107 Operating Rules](/regulations/part-107-operating-rules.md), and ATC facilities do not separate drones from other traffic inside authorized areas. The remote pilot remains responsible for yielding right of way. [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## Local Picture

For course planning around San Luis Obispo, the controlling airspace question is proximity to San Luis Obispo County Regional Airport and other local fields, which determines whether student flight sites sit in controlled airspace grids or in Class G.
