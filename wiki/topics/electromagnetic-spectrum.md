# Electromagnetic Spectrum

The **electromagnetic spectrum** is the continuous range of light across every wavelength, of which visible color occupies only a very narrow band. For advanced drone applications this matters because cameras can be built to see beyond what the human eye detects, and different wavelengths reveal different things about the world. A short tour of the spectrum pays off repeatedly across the course. [1]

## Visible Light Is a Narrow Band

What people call color is a small slice of a much longer continuous stretch of wavelengths. A camera can, in principle, respond to light well outside that slice. In practice, capturing or excluding specific wavelengths is an engineering decision, made with special pieces of glass that filter which wavelengths reach the photosensitive part of the sensor and which are blocked. Choosing what a sensor can and cannot see is central to building instruments for specific jobs, and it is the same filtering that produces color, as described in [Digital Imaging](/topics/digital-imaging.md). [1]

## Infrared

Just beyond red light is infrared, the most useful region for drone work after visible light. Its key property is the ability to distinguish photosynthetic material from non-photosynthetic material, which makes near-infrared the foundation of vegetation analysis. The applications of infrared reflectance, including the red edge and NDVI, are covered in [Multispectral Imaging](/topics/multispectral-imaging.md). [1] Much further from the visible band sits longwave infrared, which surfaces emit as a function of their own temperature rather than reflect from the sun. Cameras that image this emitted radiation, and the inspection work built on them, are covered in [Thermal Imaging](/topics/thermal-imaging.md).

## Ultraviolet

Ultraviolet light sits on the other side of the visible band and has comparatively few drone applications today, but it has genuine scientific uses worth showing students, such as revealing flower colors and snake patterns that are invisible under ordinary light. The brainstorm flags a literature search on further ultraviolet imaging applications as a task before this section is built out. [1]

## A Note on the Prefixes

The brainstorm also flags the infra and ultra prefixes for confirmation, because spectrum graphics can make their direction confusing. By convention infrared means below red in frequency, at longer wavelengths than red, and ultraviolet means beyond violet, at shorter wavelengths than violet. The two prefixes mark the regions immediately outside the red and violet ends of visible light. [1]
