# Equipment Overview

**Equipment Overview** is the program's fleet at a glance: what Cuesta College's drone program already owns, what has been recommended for purchase, and what remains proposed or under research. The program has roughly $80,000 available for equipment beyond two enterprise aircraft that arrived through separate funding [1]. Every purchasing decision now runs through two filters at once: what each course needs students to fly and process, and the tightening federal rules around Chinese-manufactured aircraft. This page maps equipment to courses and states the NDAA question honestly. The detail lives on the individual equipment pages.

## Secured

Two DJI enterprise aircraft were secured through separate funding before the main equipment budget was allocated: the [DJI Matrice 4E](dji-matrice-4e.md), a mapping platform with a mechanical shutter camera and built-in RTK, and the [DJI Matrice 4T](dji-matrice-4t.md), an inspection platform carrying a radiometric thermal camera and high-magnification zoom [1]. These two aircraft anchor the advanced courses. One caution on the record: the December 2025 equipment memo, the course outline drafts, and the planning voice memo consistently name the Matrice 4E and 4T, while an April 2026 program proposal draft describes the college-owned aircraft as a Mavic 3E and Mavic 3T, the previous generation [2]. The documents are probably describing the same purchase with the model names confused, since the Matrice 4 series replaced the Mavic 3 Enterprise series, but the actual inventory should be confirmed with Ron before it is quoted anywhere else.

Because these aircraft were purchased with California Strong Workforce Program money, which is state funding rather than a federal grant, the American Security Drone Act's procurement and operation prohibitions do not attach to them [2]. That distinction is what lets the program teach on DJI equipment at all.

## Recommended purchases

The December 2025 equipment memo to Ron recommended two packages [1]. The first is a [training fleet](training-fleet.md) of consumer DJI aircraft for AERO 100: a suggested mix of three DJI Mini 3 and three Air 3 Fly More combos with parallel chargers, a portable power station, a transport case, landing pads, and NIST test lane materials, totaling about $10,400 with tax. The second is the [Emlid RTK kit](emlid-rtk-kit.md) for the mapping course: a Reach RS4 base station at $3,900, a Reach RX2 rover at $2,400, and a $1,294 accessories kit with tripod, tribrach, survey pole, and bipod, about $7,594 before tax. Together these commit roughly $18,000 of the budget and leave the majority for software, workstations, enterprise batteries, and possible fleet diversification.

## Proposed and researched

Still open are photogrammetry and flight planning software licenses (estimated in the low thousands with educational pricing, covered in [Photogrammetry Software](../topics/photogrammetry-software.md) and [Mission Planning Software](../topics/mission-planning-software.md)), processing workstations or a cloud processing subscription, additional Matrice 4 series batteries and charging, and [field power](field-power-and-charging.md) sized for enterprise packs. The April 2026 program proposal also sketches later phases: NDAA-compliant multispectral fixed-wing aircraft and an agricultural spray platform in year two, and a dedicated public safety aircraft such as a Skydio X10 in year three [2]. The researched non-Chinese platform landscape lives on [NDAA-Compliant Platform Options](ndaa-compliant-platform-options.md).

## Equipment by course

AERO 100, the basic operator course, uses the consumer [training fleet](training-fleet.md). Small, inexpensive, self-contained aircraft with screen-equipped controllers let three to six drones fly concurrently under one instructor while limiting the cost of a student crash [1]. The enterprise aircraft can appear as demonstrations of where the career path leads.

AERO 110, drone mapping and photogrammetry, is built around the [Matrice 4E](dji-matrice-4e.md) with the [Emlid RTK kit](emlid-rtk-kit.md) supplying the base station, survey rover, and ground control workflow, plus photogrammetry software and processing hardware [3]. AERO 120, thermal imaging and applied inspection, is built around the [Matrice 4T](dji-matrice-4t.md) with thermal calibration targets and heat sources rather than heavy new aircraft spending [4].

## The NDAA question, framed honestly

Recurring feedback on the program proposal is that new aircraft should be non-Chinese, and specifically non-DJI. The bar advisors set is NDAA compliance, not necessarily Blue UAS clearance. The legal backdrop is covered in [NDAA Compliance and Blue UAS](../topics/ndaa-compliance-and-blue-uas.md): federal funds, including Perkins career and technical education money, can no longer purchase or operate DJI or Autel aircraft, while state Strong Workforce money carries no such mandate [2]. The program's stated posture in the April 2026 proposal is to recommend NDAA-compliant platforms for new purchases regardless of funding source, because Perkins-funded purchases must comply anyway, because regional employers including PG&E and Cal OES are transitioning their fleets, and because the regulatory direction is one way [2].

The honest tension is cost. The recommended DJI training fleet buys six aircraft for under $10,000; the cheapest credible NDAA-compliant trainer, the Parrot ANAFI USA, costs about $7,000 for one aircraft [5]. The program's realistic path is the one the proposal already describes: teach on the state-funded DJI equipment it owns, buy the inexpensive DJI training fleet only with non-federal money and eyes open, and add NDAA-compliant aircraft deliberately, starting with the platforms assessed on [NDAA-Compliant Platform Options](ndaa-compliant-platform-options.md), so graduates leave having flown both ecosystems.
