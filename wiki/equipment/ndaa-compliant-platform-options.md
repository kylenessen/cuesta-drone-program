# NDAA-Compliant Platform Options

**NDAA-Compliant Platform Options** is the researched answer to the program's most persistent piece of feedback: new aircraft should be non-Chinese, and specifically non-DJI. Advisors have set the bar at NDAA compliance rather than Blue UAS clearance, meaning a manufacturer attestation that no critical components come from China, with Blue listing a welcome extra, a distinction unpacked in [NDAA Compliance and Blue UAS](../topics/ndaa-compliance-and-blue-uas.md). The pressure is concrete: Perkins money cannot buy or operate DJI aircraft, regional employers led by PG&E and Cal OES are transitioning their fleets, and the FCC's December 2025 covered-list action means no new DJI models can be authorized for the US market [1] [2]. This page surveys the compliant landscape as of mid-2026 for a community college teaching program, with honest fit assessments against the DJI aircraft the program already owns. Prices are approximate, move frequently, and several vendors quote rather than list; verify everything before a purchase order.

## What the program actually needs

Three roles matter. A trainer for AERO 100 needs to be cheap enough to buy in multiples and crash without tears, with a screen-equipped controller and forgiving flight behavior. A mapping aircraft for AERO 110 needs a mechanical shutter or fast global-readout camera, RTK or PPK positioning, and terrain-following mission software, the requirements behind the [Matrice 4E](dji-matrice-4e.md). An inspection aircraft for AERO 120 needs a radiometric 640-class thermal sensor and long zoom, the [Matrice 4T](dji-matrice-4t.md) template. No compliant platform fills all three, and none matches DJI's price for any one of them. That gap is the honest starting point [1].

## Skydio X10

The X10 is the flagship American enterprise drone: Blue UAS cleared (as the X10D variant), built around onboard AI autonomy and obstacle avoidance, with swappable sensor packages combining 48 to 64 MP visual cameras, up to roughly 128x system zoom, and a Teledyne FLIR Boson+ 640 x 512 radiometric thermal sensor with 30 mK class sensitivity, better thermal sensitivity than the Matrice 4T [3]. An RTK/PPK attachment brings centimeter-level geotagging for photogrammetry [3]. Complete packages commonly land in the $15,000 to $25,000 range as of mid-2026, quote dependent [4]. Fit: this is the closest single-aircraft analog to the 4T, it is what local public safety agencies are actually buying, and the April 2026 proposal already names it for the year-three public safety course [2]. Its mapping story is credible but younger than its inspection story, and its camera is not a mechanical-shutter large sensor. As a teaching purchase it is the strongest all-around candidate.

## Inspired Flight IF800 Tomcat

The IF800 is a Blue UAS cleared medium-lift quadcopter with a 3.0 kg payload capacity on an adjustable rail, designed, manufactured, and supported in San Luis Obispo [5]. For mapping it pairs with the Sony ILX-LR1, a 61 MP full-frame industrial camera, delivering imagery no DJI integrated aircraft matches; base aircraft pricing starts around $30,000 before payload, and a complete mapping configuration reaches into the forties [6] [1]. Fit: as pure equipment it is expensive for a teaching program, but the strategic angle is unique. The manufacturer is local, the program is already recruiting an Inspired Flight contact for its advisory committee, and a partnership could mean facility tours, guest instruction, discounted or loaned aircraft, and a hiring pipeline [7]. The right first move is the relationship, not the purchase order.

## Freefly Astro and Alta X

Freefly's Astro is a US-made, Blue listed quadcopter whose mapping kit gimbals a 61 MP Sony full-frame camera (a7R IV, or the ILX-LR1 on the newer NDAA-compliant Astro Max), aimed squarely at photogrammetry; pricing is quote-based and generally reported in the twenties [8]. The larger Alta X lifts LiDAR and heavy sensors and is the common Matrice 350 replacement in compliant fleets [9]. Fit: the Astro Max mapping package is arguably the most direct compliant substitute for the Matrice 4E's job, with better imagery and triple the cost, and Freefly's open ecosystem (Auterion-based software, standard camera mounts) has teaching value in itself.

## Wingtra

WingtraOne Gen II, roughly $29,000, and the newer WingtraRAY are Swiss VTOL fixed-wing mappers, NDAA compliant and Blue listed, with PPK positioning, around an hour of endurance, and coverage rates over a thousand acres per hour, far beyond any multirotor [10]. Fit: superb for teaching large-area survey economics and fixed-wing operations, wrong as a first compliant purchase. It maps and does nothing else, and its coverage strength exceeds what campus-scale student projects need. A sensible later addition if agriculture and large-site survey work grows, alongside the fixed-wing options in the proposal's year-two plan such as the AgEagle eBee line [2].

## Parrot ANAFI USA

The ANAFI USA is the budget outlier: about $7,000, 500 g, Blue sUAS cleared, made in the USA by the French manufacturer, with 32x zoom from dual 21 MP cameras and a FLIR Boson 320 x 256 thermal sensor, 32 minutes of flight, and IP53 weather resistance [11] [12]. The April 2026 proposal names it as the example compliant trainer at $5,000 to $7,000 per unit [2]. Fit: it is the only credible compliant aircraft under $10,000 and the natural compliant trainer, but it is a 2020 design near the end of its life, some US retailers already mark it end-of-life, and its 320-class thermal is a demonstration tool rather than an inspection instrument [12]. Worth buying one or two as the compliant exposure aircraft, with confirmation of continued support first.

## Ascent AeroSystems Spirit

The Spirit is a Blue UAS cleared coaxial aircraft from Massachusetts, a rugged cylinder that packs like a water bottle, carries up to 3 kg of modular payloads, and flies about 32 minutes loaded or up to 53 minutes with double batteries; pricing is quote-only [13]. Fit: mechanically fascinating and genuinely all-weather, but payload integration is bespoke and the mapping and inspection ecosystems are thin compared to Skydio or Freefly. Better as a guest demonstration than a program purchase.

## Teal (Red Cat) Black Widow

Teal's Black Widow, built for the Army's short-range reconnaissance program, is Blue UAS certified, 3.6 lb, roughly 35 to 45 minutes of endurance, carrying a Teledyne FLIR Hadron 640R+ EO/IR package; pricing is quote-based and the product is oriented to defense and public safety ISR [14]. Fit: it teaches the defense end of the compliant market well, but it is not a mapping or inspection instrument for coursework and procurement is geared to government buyers.

## Anzu Robotics Raptor, the cautionary tale

The Raptor deserves a careful paragraph because it looks like the perfect answer and is not. Anzu licensed the DJI Mavic 3 Enterprise design and manufactured it in Malaysia, selling the Raptor at about $5,100 and the thermal Raptor T at about $7,700, undercutting every American platform [15]. But it was never NDAA compliant by most readings: the thermal sensor and roughly half the component base came from China, Anzu itself acknowledged the thermal camera likely falls outside NDAA parameters, and members of Congress raised passthrough concerns about the DJI lineage [15] [16]. It was never Blue listed. And in February 2026 Anzu ended Raptor production, citing component shortages [16]. The lesson for purchasing: made outside China is not the same as NDAA compliant, and a compliance story that depends on a licensed Chinese design is fragile both legally and commercially.

## Discontinued and adjacent

Sony's Airpeak S1, sometimes suggested as a compliant Sony-camera mapper, ended sales in March 2025 and is not a candidate [17]. Adjacent names worth knowing but not evaluated as course aircraft: BRINC (public safety interior and DFR), Vantage Robotics, Hylio (agricultural spray, named in the proposal's year-two plan), and AgEagle's eBee fixed wings [2]. The Blue UAS Cleared List changes often; verify current status at bluelist.dcma.mil before quoting compliance in any document [18].

## Recommendation tiers

Tier one, do now within the existing budget: pursue the Inspired Flight relationship through the advisory committee before any purchase, and budget one Skydio X10 with the thermal sensor package (roughly $15,000 to $25,000) as the program's first compliant aircraft. It covers inspection teaching alongside the 4T, matches what local agencies fly, carries a credible RTK mapping path, and directly answers the advisor feedback with a platform students will meet in the field.

Tier two, opportunistic: one or two Parrot ANAFI USA units as compliant trainers if end-of-life concerns check out, satisfying the proposal's compliant-trainer line at the lowest cost of entry.

Tier three, program growth: a Freefly Astro Max mapping package or an IF800 with the Sony ILX-LR1 (ideally via the local partnership) when AERO 110 needs a compliant survey aircraft, and a Wingtra when large-area or agricultural mapping enters the curriculum.

Avoid: Anzu Raptor (discontinued, compliance doubtful) and any platform whose compliance claim cannot be verified against the manufacturer's attestation or the Blue list.
