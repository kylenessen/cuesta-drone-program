# ASPRS Recommended Reading List

The **ASPRS Recommended Reading List** is the set of nine texts the ASPRS Certification and Recertification Guidelines suggest for exam preparation, assessed here as a curriculum design input for the mapping course. Because the course is being designed to teach toward the [ASPRS Certified UAS Technologist](/exams/asprs-certified-uas-technologist.md) exam, the texts ASPRS points candidates at are also a map of the body of knowledge the course should draw from, so this page treats the list as material to mine for course ideas rather than as a student purchase list. For each text it records the current edition, what the text covers, price and availability, whether a legitimate free or library option exists, and which parts map onto the course's content areas. ASPRS publishes the list with the note that the cited editions or the latest editions are acceptable [1].

Several of the citations in the manual are dated. Four of the nine texts have newer editions than the manual cites, and the manual's own entry for Lillesand and Kiefer mislabels the 2015 printing as the 5th edition when 2015 is the 7th [1] [5]. Three of the nine are out of print or unavailable from their publisher. All prices and availability on this page were checked 2026-07-10 and will drift.

## Manual of Photogrammetry

The Manual of Photogrammetry, 6th edition (J. Chris McGlone, editor in chief, ASPRS, 2013, 1,372 pages) is the comprehensive reference of the photogrammetry profession, published continuously since the 1940s. The 6th edition dropped the film and analog instrument content and rebuilt the manual around digital practice: the mathematics of perspective geometry and computer vision, statistical error modeling, coordinate transformations, optical systems and digital sensors, digital image processing, flight planning, GPS/INS integration, product formats and accuracy standards, and applications including mobile and industrial mapping [3]. It remains the current edition.

The ASPRS bookstore lists it at $175 for non-members, $125 for members, and $98 for students, but the store page is marked "This item is not available," so as of the check date it can only be bought used, where copies circulate at around $100 and up [2] [4]. For course design it is a depth reference, not a teaching text. Its error modeling and accuracy standards material sits behind [Mapping Accuracy](/topics/mapping-accuracy.md), its GPS/INS and orientation content behind [RTK and PPK Positioning](/topics/rtk-and-ppk-positioning.md) and [Structure from Motion](/topics/structure-from-motion.md), and its flight planning chapters behind [Mapping Mission Planning](/topics/mapping-mission-planning.md). It is the book to consult when a topic page needs an authoritative answer, not a book students would read.

## Remote Sensing and Image Interpretation

Remote Sensing and Image Interpretation, 7th edition (Thomas Lillesand, Ralph Kiefer, and Jonathan Chipman, Wiley, 2015, about 720 pages) is the standard introductory remote sensing textbook in American universities, and the edition the manual dates to 2015 is this one despite the "5th edition" label [1] [5]. Wiley sells the paperback at $207.95, the ebook at $136.95, and a 150 day ebook rental at $61 [5]. The Internet Archive holds a scan of the 2015 edition, but its lending status is restricted to print-disabled access, so it is not a practical free option [7]. Wiley publishes the full table of contents and the complete first chapter as free excerpts, both preserved in this wiki [6].

This is the single best match on the list for the course's remote sensing foundations. Chapter 1 covers energy sources, radiation principles, and atmospheric and surface interactions, the material behind [Electromagnetic Spectrum](/topics/electromagnetic-spectrum.md) and [Digital Imaging](/topics/digital-imaging.md). Chapter 3, Basic Principles of Photogrammetry, runs from photographic scale and relief displacement through image parallax, ground control, exterior orientation, mapping product generation, and flight planning, a compact parallel to the course's [Ground Control Points](/topics/ground-control-points.md), [Structure from Motion](/topics/structure-from-motion.md), and [Mapping Mission Planning](/topics/mapping-mission-planning.md) sequence. Chapter 4 covers multispectral, thermal, and hyperspectral sensing ([Multispectral Imaging](/topics/multispectral-imaging.md), [Thermal Imaging](/topics/thermal-imaging.md)), chapter 6 includes the principles of [Lidar](/topics/lidar.md), and chapter 7 closes with classification accuracy assessment and change detection, the conceptual ground under [Mapping Accuracy](/topics/mapping-accuracy.md) and [Change Detection and Repeat Mapping](/topics/change-detection-and-repeat-mapping.md) [6]. The satellite chapters (5 and much of 6) are the least drone-relevant portion.

## Elements of Photogrammetry with Applications in GIS

Elements of Photogrammetry with Applications in GIS, 4th edition (Paul Wolf, Bon DeWitt, and Benjamin Wilkinson, McGraw-Hill, 2013) is the standard photogrammetry course textbook, updated from the edition the manual cites to cover laser scanning and modern digital workflows. McGraw-Hill sells the print edition at $207 and the ebook at $187 [8]. It is available through the AccessEngineering subscription library, which is a question for the Cuesta library rather than a student purchase [9], and the Internet Archive holds a scan of the 2014 paperback printing with limited lending [10].

Chapter for chapter, this is the closest single text to the mapping course's photogrammetry spine. Its 21 chapters run through image measurements, vertical and tilted photo geometry, stereoscopic parallax, analytical photogrammetry, laser scanning, digital image processing fundamentals, ground control for aerial projects, aerotriangulation, and project planning, plus appendices on least squares adjustment and coordinate transformations [8]. That sequence maps directly onto [Ground Sample Distance](/topics/ground-sample-distance.md), [Ground Control Points](/topics/ground-control-points.md), [Structure from Motion](/topics/structure-from-motion.md), [Mapping Mission Planning](/topics/mapping-mission-planning.md), and [Map Projections and Coordinate Systems](/topics/map-projections-and-coordinate-systems.md). The treatment is mathematical, aimed at surveying engineering students, so the course would mine it for rigor and problem sets rather than assign it.

## Manual of Geographic Information Systems

The Manual of Geographic Information Systems (Marguerite Madden, editor, ASPRS, 2009, 1,352 pages plus DVD) is the ASPRS reference manual for GIS, 62 chapters in eight sections covering data models and metadata, data quality and uncertainty, spatio-temporal analysis, modeling, and the blending of GIS with remote sensing and GPS [11]. It has no newer edition and is showing its age, since it predates the modern cloud GIS landscape. The ASPRS store still sells it, listed at $53 as of the check date [11].

For the mapping course its relevance is narrow. The data quality and uncertainty section and the metadata chapters echo the concerns of [Mapping Accuracy](/topics/mapping-accuracy.md) and [Geospatial Data Management](/topics/geospatial-data-management.md), but a 2009 GIS manual is not where course content on today's tools should come from. It matters mainly because GIS questions appear in the exam's general knowledge areas, and the wiki already notes that students headed toward GIS as a career belong with the GISCI GISP credential rather than ASPRS [1].

## Fundamentals of Remote Sensing and Airphoto Interpretation

Fundamentals of Remote Sensing and Airphoto Interpretation, 5th edition (Thomas Avery and Graydon Berlin, Macmillan, 1992, 472 pages) is the oldest text on the list and the end of its line, with no edition after 1992. It is out of print, with used copies cheap and plentiful, and the Internet Archive scan is genuinely borrowable with a free account, the only text on the list with unrestricted controlled lending [12].

Its coverage of airphoto interpretation, the craft of reading landforms, vegetation, soils, and human features from vertical imagery, is the discipline's heritage layer, and interpretation skill still matters when a pilot reviews an orthomosaic. But everything it teaches that the course needs is taught more currently by Lillesand, Kiefer, and Chipman. The assessment here is that it stays on the list for historical completeness and costs nothing to consult, but it is not worth mining ahead of the others.

## Airborne Topographic Lidar Manual

The Airborne Topographic Lidar Manual (Michael Renslow, editor, ASPRS, 2012, 528 pages in color) is the ASPRS reference for lidar practice, covering the science behind lidar systems, mission planning, data collection and management, quality assurance, and product development, with in-depth treatments of GNSS, full waveform lidar, digital terrain modeling, sensor installation and calibration, and application chapters from flood mapping and hydro-enforcement to building extraction and transportation engineering [13]. It is the only edition, and ASPRS still sells it: hardcover at $150 non-member, $95 member, and $75 student, with a paperback and a Kindle ebook also available [13].

The mapping course teaches lidar conceptually against photogrammetry rather than as an operating skill, so this manual backs the [Lidar](/topics/lidar.md) topic page and the point cloud classification material the [ASPRS Certified UAS Technologist](/exams/asprs-certified-uas-technologist.md) matrix tests. Its GNSS and QA chapters also reinforce [GNSS Fundamentals](/topics/gnss-fundamentals.md) and [Mapping Accuracy](/topics/mapping-accuracy.md). A course copy makes sense; a student copy does not.

## Introduction to Unmanned Aircraft Systems

Introduction to Unmanned Aircraft Systems, 3rd edition (R. Kurt Barnhart, Douglas Marshall, and Eric Shappee, editors, CRC Press, 2021, 524 pages) is two editions past the 2012 printing the manual cites. Its 20 contributed chapters cover UAS history and applications, the "system" in UAS, sensing theory and practice, regulations and standards, human factors, safety assessments, export control, airframe and propulsion design, electrical systems, communications, command and control, subsystem integration, detect and avoid, public safety operations, cybersecurity and counter-UAS, unmanned traffic management, and the future of the field [14]. New hardcovers list around $180, used copies around $130, and ebook rentals around $119 through VitalSource [14] [15]. No legitimate free copy of the current edition exists.

Of the nine texts this is the one shaped most like a course, and ASPRS names it as preparation for the UAS material that makes up 70 percent of the technologist exam [1]. Its regulations, human factors, and safety assessment chapters parallel the wiki's [Part 107](/regulations/part-107.md) layer and [Aeronautical Decision-Making](/topics/aeronautical-decision-making.md). The sensing chapter parallels [Drone Payloads](/topics/drone-payloads.md), and the communications and command and control chapters parallel [Drone Radio Links and Interference](/topics/drone-radio-links-and-interference.md). Chapter topics like detect and avoid and unmanned traffic management feed the forward-looking content around [Part 108 BVLOS Rulemaking](/regulations/part-108-bvlos-rulemaking.md). This is the first text to mine for the UAS operations side of the exam.

## Introduction to UAV Systems

Introduction to UAV Systems, 5th edition (Paul Fahlstrom, Thomas Gleason, and Mohammad Sadraey, Wiley Aerospace Series, 2022, 464 pages) is one edition past the manual's citation. Wiley sells the hardcover at $159.95 and the ebook at $128, and publishes the table of contents and first chapter as free excerpts, both preserved in this wiki [16] [17]. The book is an aeronautical engineering treatment organized in six parts: introduction, the air vehicle (aerodynamics, performance, stability and control, propulsion, structures), mission planning and control stations, payloads, data links, and launch and recovery, closing with a new chapter on rotary-wing UAVs and quadcopters [17].

Its center of gravity is fixed-wing military UAV engineering, which makes it the less relevant of the two UAS texts for a mapping course flown on multirotors. The chapters that do map: mission planning and control stations sit behind [Mission Planning Software](/topics/mission-planning-software.md), the payload part behind [Drone Payloads](/topics/drone-payloads.md), the data link part behind [Drone Radio Links and Interference](/topics/drone-radio-links-and-interference.md), and the aerodynamics and performance material offers depth behind [Loading and Performance](/topics/loading-and-performance.md). Worth mining selectively for those chapters, after Barnhart.

## Glossary of the Mapping Sciences

The Glossary of the Mapping Sciences (joint publication of ASPRS, ACSM, and ASCE, 1994, 563 pages) defines more than 11,000 terms across photogrammetry, remote sensing, cartography, surveying, geodesy, hydrography, and GIS [18]. It has never been revised. The ASPRS store page lists prices of $50 non-member, $35 member, and $25 student but is marked "This item is not available," so acquisition means the used market, where copies start around $45 [18] [20]. The Internet Archive scan is restricted lending [19].

Its value to the course is as a terminology authority when writing content and exam-style questions, the same role the FAA glossaries play for the Part 107 layer. A 1994 glossary predates drones entirely, so it covers the geodesy, photogrammetry, and surveying vocabulary, not the UAS vocabulary. Useful on the shelf, cheap to acquire used, and not a mining priority.

## Reading Priorities for Course Design

Read as a curriculum input, the list sorts into three tiers. The core tier is Lillesand, Kiefer, and Chipman for remote sensing foundations, Wolf, DeWitt, and Wilkinson for the photogrammetry spine, and Barnhart for the UAS operations layer, together covering nearly everything the exam matrix tests and most of what the mapping course teaches. The reference tier is the three ASPRS manuals and the glossary, consulted when topic pages need authoritative depth on accuracy standards, lidar practice, or terminology, with the lidar manual the most likely course purchase since ASPRS still sells it at a member price. The historical tier is Avery and Berlin, free to borrow and safely skipped. Fahlstrom and Gleason sits between tiers, worth selective mining for payload, data link, and mission planning chapters.

The gap the list itself reveals is also worth noting. Nothing on it covers small-drone photogrammetry as actually practiced, with RTK multirotors, structure from motion software, and ground control workflows, because the newest text is from 2022 and the mapping-focused ones predate the drone era. The course's field practice content will have to come from the wiki's own topic layer and current industry sources, with these texts supplying the underlying theory.
