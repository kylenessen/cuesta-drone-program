# Part 107 Coverage of the AERO 100 Outline

**Part 107 Coverage of the AERO 100 Outline** asks two mirrored questions: whether the approved AERO 100 course outline covers everything the [Part 107 Knowledge Test](../exams/part-107-knowledge-test.md) requires, and whether the wiki covers everything the approved Course Outline of Record requires. The short answers: the outline's nine content areas map cleanly onto the five ACS test areas with no missing subject, though the 2021 regulatory additions (Remote ID, operations over people, and the night rules) are not called out explicitly and the test's weighting suggests where lecture hours should concentrate; and, following a bullet-by-bullet audit against the extracted COR text, every COR topic and sub-bullet now maps to a wiki page or a substantial section of one, as tabulated below.

## Question

AERO 100's first student learning outcome requires students to pass a written Part 107-style exam at 80 percent, above the FAA's 70 percent bar. Does the approved topical outline, written before course content development, fully cover the tested material as defined in [FAA-S-ACS-10B](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)? [1](../sources/aero-100-course-outline.md) [2](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

## Method

Each ACS Area of Operation and Task was compared against the outline's nine content areas as recorded in the [AERO 100 Course Outline](../sources/aero-100-course-outline.md) anchor page. The comparison used the April 2021 ACS, which is current as of this writing. [2](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) The reverse audit, wiki against COR, worked from the full extracted CurriQunet All Fields report, checking each of the nine topics and every sub-bullet against the wiki's pages and sections and adding pages or sections where coverage was missing or thin. [1](../sources/aero-100-course-outline.md)

## Findings

The mapping is solid at the subject level. ACS Area I (Regulations) lands in the outline's regulations and references unit. Area II (Airspace) lands in airspace and navigation, which already names charts, classifications, and special use airspace. Area III (Weather) is the outline's strongest match; its aviation weather unit lists fronts, stability, thunderstorms, fog, icing, and weather sources almost verbatim from the ACS. Area IV (Loading and Performance) lands in aircraft performance, which names weight and balance and density altitude. Area V (Operations) spreads across four outline units: emergency procedures, human factors, maintenance and preflight inspection, and airport and off-airport operations, with [Radio Communications](../topics/radio-communications.md) implicitly under airport operations. [1](../sources/aero-100-course-outline.md) [2](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

Three development priorities follow. First, the 2021 additions need explicit slots: [Remote Identification](../regulations/remote-identification.md) is a full ACS task, so the course planning note that treats it as historical context understates it; [Operations Over People](../regulations/operations-over-people.md) categories and [Certificate of Waiver](../regulations/certificate-of-waiver.md) policy are likewise tested elements the outline never names. They fit naturally inside the regulations unit. Second, weighting: Operations (35 to 45 percent) plus Regulations (15 to 25 percent) form the bulk of the test, so lecture and quiz time should tilt toward those areas rather than dividing evenly across the nine outline units. Third, chart reading is a skill, not a topic: since airspace questions arrive as [Sectional Chart](../topics/sectional-chart.md) excerpts, students need repeated practice with testing-supplement figures, which argues for chart exercises threaded through several weeks rather than one navigation lecture. [2](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

The 80 percent course standard above the FAA's 70 percent is an asset: students who clear the course bar have margin on test day, and the standard justifies the extra practice testing.

## COR-to-Wiki Mapping

The approved COR's nine topics and every sub-bullet under them were audited against the wiki in July 2026 using the full extracted CurriQunet report. [1](../sources/aero-100-course-outline.md) The audit closed its gaps by adding three pages ([Notices to Airmen (NOTAMs)](../topics/notams.md), [Aeronautical Publications and References](../topics/aeronautical-publications-and-references.md), and [UAV Flight Operations](../topics/uav-flight-operations.md)) and new sections on postflight inspection, ground effect and performance, structural icing and frost, wind drift correction, geographical coordinates, off-airport operations, and emergency communications to existing pages. ## Caveats

The ACS weighting percentages describe the FAA test, not necessarily the best pedagogical order. The types of UAV cell, formerly the thinnest mapping, is now carried by the Types, Components, and Uses section of [Uncrewed Aircraft](../topics/uncrewed-aircraft.md), which surveys airframe types (multirotor, fixed-wing, hybrid VTOL, single-rotor) and core components against the Remote Pilot Study Guide. The pending [Part 108 BVLOS Rulemaking](../regulations/part-108-bvlos-rulemaking.md) could eventually change the tested material; the ACS in force should be rechecked each time the course is revised, and the NOTAM page's access details should be rechecked after the FAA completes its NOTAM system transition in 2026.
