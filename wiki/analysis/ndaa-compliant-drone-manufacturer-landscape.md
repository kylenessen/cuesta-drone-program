# NDAA-Compliant Drone Manufacturer Landscape

The **NDAA-Compliant Drone Manufacturer Landscape** surveys the companies, as opposed to the individual aircraft, building NDAA-compliant quadcopters and hexacopters suited to the program's mapping, inspection, and agricultural teaching, as of July 2026. It answers two program decisions: which manufacturers deserve a purchase quote under a limited budget, and which could contribute a member to the advisory committee. The headline local finding is that Inspired Flight Technologies is the only commercial multirotor manufacturer in San Luis Obispo County. The county's other two UAS companies, Zone 5 Technologies and Edge Autonomy, are defense contractors that do not sell classroom-suitable aircraft, and both were acquired by larger defense firms within the past eighteen months. This page is the company-level companion to [NDAA-Compliant Platform Options](../equipment/ndaa-compliant-platform-options.md), which assesses individual aircraft against course needs.

## Question

Which companies manufacture NDAA-compliant multirotors relevant to mapping, inspection, and agricultural spray coursework, how commercially healthy is each, which have a presence in San Luis Obispo County or nearby counties, and what does the answer imply for the program's next aircraft purchase and its advisory committee recruitment?

## Method

The findings come from a July 2026 web survey of manufacturer product pages, Blue UAS Cleared List coverage, and industry press, cross-checked against the repository's advisory committee notes and the wiki's existing NDAA pages. Prices are approximate, most vendors quote rather than list, and this market has been reshaped by acquisitions and shutdowns within single years, so every company-status claim here carries a short shelf life. Verify before quoting in a purchase order or an invitation letter.

## Shape of the compliant market

The compliant market in mid-2026 has three defining features. First, the regulatory floor keeps rising: DJI and Autel were added to the FCC covered list effective December 2025, blocking new equipment authorizations, which pushes even buyers with no federal money toward the compliant vendors [1]. Second, the Blue UAS Cleared List, now managed by the Defense Contract Management Agency, has grown past fifty platforms from more than thirty manufacturers, so compliance shopping is no longer a two-vendor exercise [2] [3]. Third, the sector is consolidating and shaking out. Robinson Helicopter acquired Ascent AeroSystems in April 2024 [4], Redwire acquired Edge Autonomy in a $925 million deal announced January 2025 [5], and Kongsberg acquired Zone 5 Technologies in December 2025 [6]. On the failure side, Watts Innovations closed in July 2024 [7], Guardian Agriculture shut down in 2025 after failing to raise funding [8], Sony ended Airpeak sales in March 2025 [9], and Anzu Robotics ended Raptor production in February 2026 [10]. The purchasing lesson is that vendor viability is a selection criterion equal to specifications. A compliant drone from a company that folds becomes an unsupported aircraft with no parts channel, so companies with production scale, revenue, or deep-pocketed owners deserve preference.

## Established platform makers

Six companies anchor the compliant multirotor market. Their aircraft are assessed individually in [NDAA-Compliant Platform Options](../equipment/ndaa-compliant-platform-options.md), so the treatment here is company health and character.

**Skydio** (San Mateo, California) is the largest US drone manufacturer, built around autonomy software, with the X10 as its enterprise flagship and the X10D on the Blue list [11]. It dominates the public safety and utility markets the program's courses feed, and complete packages run roughly $15,000 to $25,000 [12]. **Freefly Systems** (Woodinville, Washington) came from the cinema world and now fields the Astro and NDAA-compliant Astro Max quadcopters for mapping plus the Blue-listed Alta X heavy lifter [13]. **Inspired Flight Technologies** (San Luis Obispo, California) builds the IF800 Tomcat quadcopter and IF1200A heavy-lift hexacopter, both dual-certified under Blue UAS and AUVSI Green UAS, with a 2026 RTK and PPK module for mapping work [14]. **Parrot** (French, with US operations in Boston) sells the ANAFI USA, the only credible compliant aircraft under $10,000, though the design dates to 2020 and some retailers flag it end-of-life [15]. **Ascent AeroSystems** (Tewksbury, Massachusetts) builds coaxial aircraft and now operates as a Robinson Helicopter subsidiary with manufacturing capacity at Robinson's Torrance, California plant, which resolves the viability question a niche design would otherwise raise [4]. **Red Cat Holdings** and its Teal Drones unit (Salt Lake City, Utah) build Blue-listed reconnaissance quadcopters aimed at defense and public safety procurement rather than commercial mapping [16].

## Mid-market challengers

Below the majors sits a tier of smaller American manufacturers, several of which are more interesting for a teaching budget than the flagships.

**Skyfish** (Stevensville, Montana) launched the Osprey at XPONENTIAL 2025 as a direct American answer to DJI's enterprise line: Blue UAS cleared, Green UAS certified, carrying the Sony ILX-LR1 61 megapixel full-frame camera with RTK, marketed for survey-grade inspection and photogrammetry at $25,000 to $30,000 for a complete system [17] [18]. That undercuts an equivalent Inspired Flight or Freefly mapping configuration substantially while flying the same class of camera, and it makes Skyfish the most direct compliant mapping competitor to the program's [DJI Matrice 4E](../equipment/dji-matrice-4e.md) template. Endurance is the tradeoff, about 30 minutes as shipped [17].

**Vision Aerial** (Bozeman, Montana) may be the most budget-relevant discovery in this survey. It manufactures in Montana and sells the SwitchBlade-Elite folding tricopter at about $14,000 and the Vector hexacopter, with roughly 40 minutes of endurance and multi-payload capacity, at about $21,000 before payload [19] [20]. The company markets to universities and public agencies and meets Buy American sourcing thresholds, though its aircraft are not on the Blue list, so compliance rests on the manufacturer's NDAA attestation [19]. For a program that wants students flying an American hexacopter without a $30,000 commitment, the Vector is the price point to quote.

**Harris Aerial** (Orlando, Florida) builds the Carrier family of heavy-lift electric and hybrid multirotors and joined the Blue UAS Cleared List in June 2026 [21]. It plays in the same heavy-lift LiDAR territory as the IF1200A and Alta X. **Easy Aerial** (Brooklyn, New York) builds Blue-listed hexacopters centered on autonomous and tethered monitoring [3]. **Vantage Robotics** (San Leandro, California) makes the Blue-listed Vesper for government reconnaissance [3]. **BRINC Drones** (Seattle, Washington) is the public safety specialist, building NDAA-compliant interior tactical drones and the Responder drone-as-first-responder platform, and its national push with the National League of Cities makes it the name students will meet in DFR programs [22]. **ModalAI** (San Diego, California) sells Blue Framework autopilots and developer drones rather than field-ready mapping aircraft, relevant if the curriculum ever adds a build-a-drone robotics unit [23].

## Agricultural spray manufacturers

The spray market is the starkest compliance story because DJI's Agras line and XAG owned it almost entirely, and the FCC action now chokes the supply of new Chinese models [1] [24]. **Hylio** (Richmond, Texas) is the main American beneficiary and effectively the only major NDAA-compliant spray platform with US-based service. Its AG series runs from the AG-110, starting around $17,000, to the 18-gallon AG-272 at roughly $56,000 base and $72,000 with batteries and charger, all flown through its AgroSol software, which lets one operator run a swarm of up to eight aircraft [24] [25]. The company assembles everything in Texas, claims capacity of 5,000 aircraft per year, and in 2026 introduced the larger HYL-150 Ares and HYL-300 [24] [26]. The cautionary tale on this side of the market is Guardian Agriculture, which won the first US eVTOL operating authorization for its SC1 sprayer and still shut down in 2025 with one paying customer [8]. If the ag spray track championed in the program's proposal work moves forward, the Hylio AG-110 is the teachable entry point, and the regulatory stack it triggers is already documented in [Part 137 Agricultural Operations](../regulations/part-137-agricultural-operations.md) and [California Pesticide Licensing](../regulations/california-pesticide-licensing.md). For non-spray agricultural work, scouting and multispectral mapping need no dedicated ag aircraft, since sensor makers like Sentera integrate onto the compliant multirotors above [23].

## San Luis Obispo County and the Central Coast

San Luis Obispo has a genuine UAS cluster, but only one company in it sells what the program teaches with.

### Inspired Flight Technologies

Founded in 2017 in San Luis Obispo, Inspired Flight is the county's commercial drone manufacturer, building the IF800 and IF1200A for mapping, inspection, and government customers, with both platforms dual-certified Blue and Green UAS [14] [27]. The advisory committee recruitment is already underway: Sierra Martin, Flight Operations Coordinator and Project Manager, is the confirmed target contact, with Adam Bilmes, Keith Gerhardt, and CEO Rick Stollmeyer as backups [28]. The company remains the program's most natural manufacturer partner on pure geography and product fit, and a partnership could plausibly include facility tours, guest instruction, and equipment access, which matters because its aircraft are otherwise priced above a teaching budget [29].

### Zone 5 Technologies

Zone 5, founded 2011 and based in San Luis Obispo, grew to roughly 250 employees and over $100 million in 2025 revenue as a Department of Defense prime contractor. Its Paladin quadcopter was the first counter-UAS interceptor added to the Blue UAS Cleared List, and in December 2025 Kongsberg Defence and Aerospace agreed to acquire the company, positioning it for missile production [6] [30]. Zone 5 sells nothing a community college course would fly. Its relevance is as a local employer: a large, growing aerospace manufacturer in town that hires technicians, pilots, and engineers, and therefore a plausible advisory committee seat representing the defense career pathway rather than the equipment market.

### Edge Autonomy

Edge Autonomy is headquartered in San Luis Obispo, formed in 2021 from the merger of UAV Factory and Jennings Aeronautics, and builds the Penguin and Stalker fixed-wing ISR aircraft used by US and allied militaries, including in Ukraine. Redwire acquired it in a $925 million deal announced January 2025 [5]. Like Zone 5, it offers no classroom multirotor, but it is a substantial local manufacturing employer in the same hiring category.

### Nearby counties

No second Inspired Flight exists in the surrounding counties. **AeroVironment** (Simi Valley, Ventura County) is the region's giant, but it builds military small UAS and loitering munitions, not commercial multirotors [31]. **Parallel Flight Technologies** (La Selva Beach, Santa Cruz County) builds the Firefly, a hybrid gas-electric heavy lifter carrying 100 pounds for over an hour, and received its FAA Section 44807 exemption for commercial operations in March 2026. Its wildfire logistics focus makes it a regional company worth knowing if the public safety course matures, though its aircraft is far from a classroom trainer [32] [33]. Beyond manufacturers, the regional drone economy is service providers, aerial photography, inspection, and mapping operators, which are potential employer seats but outside this page's manufacturer scope.

## Advisory committee implications

The manufacturer seat has one obvious occupant. Inspired Flight is local, commercial, product-aligned with the curriculum, and already being recruited through Sierra Martin [28]. The company-level survey does not change that answer, but it does provide a hedge: if the relationship underdelivers, the program is not without options. Skydio's regional presence runs through its customers rather than an office, and Madison Muschetto's agency transition to the Skydio X10 illustrates that a local public-safety operator can represent the dominant compliant platform's perspective without a manufacturer on the roster [28]. Zone 5 under Kongsberg and Edge Autonomy under Redwire both represent the defense manufacturing career path, and a seat from either would speak for a hiring pipeline rather than an equipment vendor. Parallel Flight is a longer-shot regional innovator seat tied to the wildfire and public safety direction. Hylio has no California presence, so the ag spray perspective is better represented by an applicator or dealer than by the manufacturer.

## Purchase implications

The recommendation tiers in [NDAA-Compliant Platform Options](../equipment/ndaa-compliant-platform-options.md) stand, and this survey sharpens them in three places. First, any Skydio X10 quote should be accompanied by a Skyfish Osprey quote, since the Osprey delivers a 61 megapixel full-frame mapping camera at a similar or lower price and the comparison will reveal whether the program values inspection autonomy or mapping imagery more [12] [17]. Second, Vision Aerial's Vector at about $21,000 is the cheapest credible American hexacopter and belongs in any conversation about compliant aircraft students actually handle, with the caveat that its compliance is attestation-based rather than Blue-listed [19] [20]. Third, if the spray track is funded, the Hylio AG-110 at around $17,000 is the only realistic compliant trainer for it [25]. Across all three, the viability screen from the market survey applies: prefer Blue-listed platforms and companies with scale or strong owners, and treat single-product startups as rental or partnership candidates rather than purchases.
