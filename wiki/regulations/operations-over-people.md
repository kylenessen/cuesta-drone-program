# Operations Over People

**Operations Over People** is the framework in [Part 107](part-107.md) Subpart E, effective April 2021, that lets remote pilots fly over human beings without a waiver by using aircraft that meet one of four escalating eligibility categories. Before this rule, section 107.39 flatly prohibited flight over anyone not directly participating in the operation or under a covered structure or stationary vehicle; that prohibition still stands as the default, and the categories are the exceptions to it. The 2021 ACS revision added a full task on this material, so it is tested knowledge, not an advanced elective. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

People who are direct participants (the remote PIC, a person manipulating the controls, a visual observer, or crew necessary to safety) may always be overflown, as may anyone under a safe cover such as a structure or stationary vehicle. Everything else requires operating within a category. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## Requirements

Category 1 uses aircraft weighing 0.55 pounds (250 grams) or less at takeoff, including everything on board or attached, with no exposed rotating parts that would lacerate skin on impact. There is no application or declaration process; the remote pilot simply verifies the aircraft qualifies. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

Category 2 and Category 3 use heavier aircraft that satisfy performance-based injury severity limits, demonstrated through an FAA-accepted Means of Compliance (MoC) and documented by the manufacturer on an FAA-accepted Declaration of Compliance (DoC). The aircraft must carry a label identifying its category, and the remote pilot must verify the DoC is listed as valid on the FAA's database and follow the manufacturer's remote pilot operating instructions. Category 3 aircraft tolerate a higher injury threshold, and in exchange Category 3 operations are limited to closed or restricted access sites where everyone is on notice, or to operations that avoid sustained flight over any person not participating or under cover. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

Category 4 uses aircraft with an airworthiness certificate issued under Part 21, operated within the limits of the FAA-approved flight manual and maintained under the manufacturer's instructions, which is effectively a manned-aviation certification regime applied to a drone. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

Sustained flight over an open-air assembly of people carries an extra condition: for Category 1, 2, and 4 the aircraft must comply with standard [Remote Identification](remote-identification.md), and Category 3 operations over open-air assemblies are prohibited outright. A brief, incidental transit over part of a gathering is not sustained flight. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

Operations over moving vehicles follow the same categories with a further restriction: within a closed or restricted access site the people in vehicles must be on notice, and outside such a site only transiting flight is allowed, never sustained hover over traffic. Flying over people at night combines this rule with the [Night Operations](../topics/night-operations.md) requirements. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

Part 107 sets no fixed standoff distance from bystanders. The remote pilot is expected to choose a minimum distance based on aircraft performance, wind, the movement of people, probable failure modes, and pilot proficiency, which makes this a good classroom risk-assessment exercise. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)
