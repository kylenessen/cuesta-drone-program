# Part 107 Operating Rules

The **Part 107 Operating Rules** are the flight rules in Subpart B of [Part 107](part-107.md) that govern every small UAS operation, from who is responsible for the flight to how high, how fast, and in what weather a drone may fly. They are the most heavily tested regulatory material on the [Part 107 Knowledge Test](../exams/part-107-knowledge-test.md), and the ACS enumerates them item by item in its Operating Rules task. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

For teaching purposes the rules cluster into four groups: the remote pilot in command's responsibility, the see-and-avoid rules, the numeric operating limitations, and the categorical prohibitions.

## Remote Pilot in Command

Every flight must have a designated remote pilot in command (remote PIC) who holds a [Remote Pilot Certificate](remote-pilot-certificate.md) and who is directly responsible for, and the final authority on, the operation. A person without a certificate may manipulate the controls only under the direct supervision of a remote PIC who can take immediate control. The remote PIC must ensure the aircraft is in a condition for safe operation and must perform the preflight familiarization and inspection required by section 107.49, including assessing the operating environment, briefing all participants, and checking the aircraft and control links. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

In an in-flight emergency, the remote PIC may deviate from any rule of Part 107 to the extent needed to meet the emergency, and must submit a written report to the FAA if requested. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md) The operational side of this authority is covered under [Emergency Procedures](../topics/emergency-procedures.md).

## Seeing and Avoiding

Section 107.31 requires the aircraft to remain within unaided visual line of sight (VLOS) of the remote PIC or a visual observer, with vision correctable only by ordinary glasses or contact lenses; first-person-view cameras do not satisfy the requirement. If a visual observer is used under section 107.33, the observer and remote PIC must maintain effective communication and the remote PIC retains the ability to see the aircraft. Small unmanned aircraft must always yield the right of way to all other aircraft, and section 107.37 obliges the remote pilot to see and avoid manned traffic rather than expect to be seen. One remote pilot may operate only one small unmanned aircraft at a time under section 107.35. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## Operating Limitations

Section 107.51 sets the numeric limits that anchor many test questions. Groundspeed may not exceed 87 knots, which is 100 miles per hour. Altitude may not exceed 400 feet above ground level, unless the aircraft stays within a 400-foot radius of a structure and no more than 400 feet above the structure's immediate uppermost limit. Minimum flight visibility is 3 statute miles from the control station, and the aircraft must stay at least 500 feet below and 2,000 feet horizontally from clouds. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

Daylight operation is the default, but since the 2021 amendments a remote pilot with current training may fly at night or during civil twilight if the aircraft carries anti-collision lighting visible for 3 statute miles; details are on [Night Operations](../topics/night-operations.md). Operations in controlled airspace require prior authorization under section 107.41, covered in [Airspace Authorization](../topics/airspace-authorization.md), and flight in prohibited or restricted areas or under a NOTAM-designated flight restriction is barred without permission. [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## Prohibitions and Special Cases

Several rules are categorical. No careless or reckless operation, and no dropping an object in a way that creates an undue hazard. No carriage of hazardous materials. No operation over people or moving vehicles except within the [Operations Over People](operations-over-people.md) category system. No operation from a moving aircraft at all, and operation from a moving land or water vehicle only over sparsely populated areas and never for compensation-based property transport. Transportation of property for compensation or hire is allowed within a state, but the total weight including cargo must stay under 55 pounds and the flight must remain within visual line of sight. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

The alcohol and drug provisions mirror manned aviation: no acting as a crewmember within 8 hours of consuming alcohol, with a blood alcohol concentration of 0.04 percent or greater, or while using any drug that affects mental or physical capability. Refusing a lawful alcohol test or refusing to furnish results is grounds for certificate denial or revocation. The physiological reasoning behind these limits is covered in [Remote Pilot Physiology](../topics/remote-pilot-physiology.md). [2](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

Small unmanned aircraft may not operate with an ATC transponder or ADS-B Out enabled unless specifically authorized, a 2021 addition that surprises students coming from manned aviation. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

Most of these rules can be waived individually through a [Certificate of Waiver](certificate-of-waiver.md) when an applicant demonstrates equivalent safety.
