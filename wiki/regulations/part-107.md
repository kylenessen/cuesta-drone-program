# Part 107

**Part 107** of Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations is the FAA's small unmanned aircraft systems rule, the primary regulation governing civil drone operations in the United States. It applies to the operation of small unmanned aircraft, meaning aircraft weighing less than 55 pounds at takeoff including everything on board or attached, and it created the [Remote Pilot Certificate](remote-pilot-certificate.md) that authorizes commercial drone flight. [1](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

The rule took effect in August 2016 and was substantially amended in 2021, when the FAA added routine [Night Operations](../topics/night-operations.md), [Operations Over People](operations-over-people.md), and, in companion Part 89, [Remote Identification](remote-identification.md). The [Part 107 Knowledge Test](../exams/part-107-knowledge-test.md) draws its Regulations content area directly from this rule, so students need a working command of its structure and definitions, not just its flight limits.

## Structure

Part 107 is organized into subparts. Subpart A covers applicability, definitions, and general obligations such as accident reporting and inspection. Subpart B contains the [Part 107 Operating Rules](part-107-operating-rules.md), the day-to-day flight rules including visual line of sight, operating limitations, and airspace requirements. Subpart C governs remote pilot certification, including eligibility, the knowledge test, and knowledge recency. Subpart D establishes the [Certificate of Waiver](certificate-of-waiver.md) mechanism for deviating from specific rules. Subpart E, added in 2021, sets the category system for [Operations Over People](operations-over-people.md). [1](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

Part 107 does not stand alone. Aircraft must be registered under Part 47 or Part 48 before operating (see [Drone Registration](drone-registration.md)), and most must broadcast [Remote Identification](remote-identification.md) under Part 89. Recreational flyers may instead operate under the statutory exception for limited recreational operations, which is a separate framework with its own rules; Part 107 is the default that applies whenever an operation does not fully satisfy that exception. Beyond visual line of sight flying, currently possible only by waiver, is the subject of pending [Part 108 BVLOS Rulemaking](part-108-bvlos-rulemaking.md). [1](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## Accident Reporting

Section 107.9 requires the remote pilot in command to report an accident to the FAA within 10 calendar days when the operation causes serious injury to any person or any loss of consciousness, or when it causes damage to property other than the drone itself exceeding $500 to repair or replace (whichever is lower). Reports can be filed with the nearest FSDO or through the FAA's online portal. The AERO 100 scenario worth teaching: shattering a $200 window is not reportable, but knocking someone unconscious always is. [1](../sources/ac-107-2a.md)

Falsification, reproduction, or alteration of certificates, records, or reports is grounds for denial, suspension, or revocation of a certificate or waiver, a point the ACS tests under its General regulations task. [1](../sources/ac-107-2a.md) [2](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)
