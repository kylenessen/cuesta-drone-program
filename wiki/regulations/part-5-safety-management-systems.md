# Part 5 Safety Management Systems

Part 5 of Title 14 establishes the FAA's organizational Safety Management System requirements. An SMS gives an organization a repeatable way to identify hazards, decide how to control risk, and verify that its controls work. For Cuesta, it is a strong framework for teaching professional safety practice. It is not a blanket legal requirement imposed on every drone pilot. This article was checked September 22, 2026 against the [current eCFR capture](../sources/14-cfr-part-5.md).

## What applies to drones

Section 5.1 applies to specified certificate holders and applicants. It covers Part 121 and Part 135 operators, holders and applicants for § 91.147 air tour authorizations, and the specified Part 21 design and production certificate relationships. Ordinary Part 107 operations are not listed. A remote pilot certificate or a classroom drone build does not, by itself, place a person in those categories. A drone operator holding or applying for the relevant Part 135 authority is different. Applicability follows its operating authority, not simply the fact that it flies an unmanned aircraft. [Part 5, § 5.1](../sources/14-cfr-part-5.md)

For Part 135 operators authorized before May 28, 2024, § 5.9(a) sets May 28, 2027 as the deadline for implementation and a declaration of compliance. Section 5.9(b) addresses applicants on or after May 28, 2024 and pending applications. These dates are not deadlines for an ordinary Part 107 class. Actual certification cases also require checking current FAA implementation guidance. [Part 5, § 5.9](../sources/14-cfr-part-5.md)

Cuesta can adopt SMS practices voluntarily. [AC 120-92D](../sources/ac-120-92d.md) expressly supports voluntary development and scales implementation to the organization. Teaching with this framework does not establish FAA acceptance, SMS Voluntary Program enrollment, or full Part 5 compliance. Formal recognition would be a separate institutional project. The legal operating requirements for each flight still come from its applicable authority, including [Part 107](part-107.md), registration, Remote ID, and any authorization or waiver conditions. A favorable risk score cannot authorize a prohibited flight.

## How the requested references fit together

| Reference | Role | Teaching use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [14 CFR Part 5](../sources/14-cfr-part-5.md) | U.S. regulation for the organizations in § 5.1 | Establish the requirements and distinguish applicability from voluntary adoption. |
| [AC 120-92D](../sources/ac-120-92d.md) | FAA implementation guidance | Explain methods and adapt Appendix G for a small operation. |
| [ICAO Annex 19](../sources/icao-annex-19.md) | International standards and recommended practices implemented through States | Explain the international framework and its relationship to national rules. |
| [ICAO Doc 9859](../sources/icao-doc-9859.md) | Safety management implementation manual | Provide deeper reading on culture, performance, and SMS implementation. |

ICAO is the International Civil Aviation Organization, the publisher of the last two references. A State safety programme belongs to a country. An operator SMS belongs to an organization. The FAA circular's Appendix A connects the ICAO SMS framework to Part 5. Advisory guidance and international standards should not be presented as interchangeable with directly applicable U.S. operating rules.

## The structure to teach

Section 5.5 names four components and requires the system to suit the organization's size, scope, and complexity. Subpart B, safety policy, establishes objectives, resources, reporting expectations, accountability, a code of ethics, and emergency coordination. Subpart C, safety risk management, describes systems, identifies hazards, evaluates risk, and develops controls. Subpart D, safety assurance, gathers and analyzes operating evidence, evaluates controls, and corrects deficiencies. Subpart E, safety promotion, covers training and communication. Subpart F adds documentation and records. [Part 5, §§ 5.5 and 5.21 through 5.97](../sources/14-cfr-part-5.md)

The separation between risk management and assurance matters. Before introducing a new aircraft or procedure, assess what might go wrong. After using it, examine whether the controls actually worked. Section 5.73 sends new hazards and ineffective controls back through risk management. The [SMS topic](../topics/safety-management-systems.md) translates that cycle into drone practice.

## Details that a classroom summary can miss

The accountable executive must have operational authority, control over financial and human resources, and ultimate safety responsibility under § 5.25. Naming a student safety officer does not create that authority. For a voluntary college program, identify the actual institutional decision maker before describing anyone as its accountable executive.

Section 5.57 requires hazard notification to interfacing persons who could address the hazard or reduce its risk. Section 5.71 includes investigation of incoming external hazard notifications and a confidential employee reporting system. The college adaptation should therefore teach communication with site owners, other crews, and maintainers as well as internal reporting. Confidential reporting is not a promise of anonymity, immunity, or exemption from required reporting.

Section 5.97 specifies different retention periods. Risk management outputs remain as long as a control is relevant. Safety assurance outputs remain at least five years. Training records remain during employment. Safety communications under §§ 5.93 and 5.57 remain at least 24 consecutive calendar months. These are regulatory requirements for covered organizations, not automatically the college's student-record policy. The college needs an adopted retention and access policy if it builds a real reporting system.

The limited single-person exceptions in § 5.9(e) concern an organization in which one pilot performs all relevant functions. A class with one instructor and many students should not assume that exception applies. [Part 5, §§ 5.9, 5.25, 5.57, 5.71 and 5.97](../sources/14-cfr-part-5.md)

## ICAO edition transition

Annex 19's third edition was published in November 2025 and supersedes the previous editions on November 26, 2026. Its Amendment 2 became effective November 4, 2025, but effectiveness and applicability are different dates. As of this article's September 2026 check, teach the second edition as the applicable baseline and identify the third edition as the forthcoming change. [ICAO edition record](../sources/icao-annex-19.md)

ICAO identifies expansion to certified RPAS operators authorized for international operations and approved maintenance organizations serving them. That is useful career context. It does not show that every domestic Part 107 operator becomes subject to Part 5 in November. Before teaching after the transition, recheck the applicable Annex edition and U.S. rules. The [Doc 9859 record](../sources/icao-doc-9859.md) separately identifies the fourth edition of the manual as the edition verified in this research.
