# Part 108 BVLOS Rulemaking

The **Part 108 BVLOS Rulemaking** is the FAA's in-progress effort to normalize beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS) drone operations, published as a notice of proposed rulemaking titled Normalizing Unmanned Aircraft Systems Beyond Visual Line of Sight Operations on August 7, 2025. Today, flying beyond visual line of sight requires waiving section 107.31 through a [Certificate of Waiver](/regulations/certificate-of-waiver.md); Part 108 would replace that per-operation waiver system with a standing regulatory framework. For curriculum planning it is the largest regulatory change on the horizon, though as of July 2026 it remains a proposal, and course content should continue to teach the visual line of sight rule as law. [1](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/28/2026-01644/normalizing-unmanned-aircraft-systems-beyond-visual-line-of-sight-operations-reopening-of-comment) [2](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/BVLOS_NPRM_website_version.pdf)

## Requirements

As proposed, the rule would create two levels of approval, permitted operations for lower-risk cases and an operational certificate for higher-risk ones, with risk tiers keyed to the population density of the operating area. It would approve operational areas rather than individual flights, and it introduces new operational roles beyond the Part 107 remote pilot in command. The proposal's most contested element would adjust right-of-way expectations between unmanned and low-flying manned aircraft, a departure from the categorical yield rule in the current [Part 107 Operating Rules](/regulations/part-107-operating-rules.md). These provisions describe the proposal, not current law, and any of them may change in the final rule. [2](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/BVLOS_NPRM_website_version.pdf) [3](https://dronexl.co/2026/06/18/faa-part-108-presumptive-right-of-way-bvlos-delay/)

## Status

The initial comment period closed October 6, 2025. The FAA reopened the comment record on January 28, 2026, with comments due by February 11, 2026. A June 2025 executive order had directed a final rule by early 2026, and industry press reported that deadline passing without publication; as of the last check of this page, no final rule has been published. This page carries a `last_checked` date and should be reverified each term before Part 108 claims appear in course materials. [1](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/28/2026-01644/normalizing-unmanned-aircraft-systems-beyond-visual-line-of-sight-operations-reopening-of-comment) [3](https://dronexl.co/2026/06/18/faa-part-108-presumptive-right-of-way-bvlos-delay/)

The curriculum implication is timing. If a final rule lands during a course development cycle, the advanced course content on waivers and BVLOS-adjacent work (mapping large sites, linear inspections) may need revision, while the AERO 100 knowledge-test content stays anchored to Part 107 until the FAA revises the [Part 107 Knowledge Test](/exams/part-107-knowledge-test.md) and its ACS.
