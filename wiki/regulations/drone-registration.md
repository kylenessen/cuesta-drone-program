# Drone Registration

**Drone Registration** is the requirement that a small unmanned aircraft be registered with the FAA before it operates under [Part 107](/regulations/part-107.md). Part 48 provides the streamlined online option through the FAADroneZone portal for aircraft operated only within the United States, and Part 47 remains available as the paper process used for manned aircraft. Registration is aircraft-side compliance, parallel to the pilot-side [Remote Pilot Certificate](/regulations/remote-pilot-certificate.md), and the knowledge test expects students to know who must register, how, and what the recreational exemption does and does not cover. [1](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

## Requirements

Registration under Part 107 costs $5 per aircraft and is valid for three years. Recreational flyers operating under the statutory exception instead pay $5 for one registration number that covers every drone in their inventory. The two registration types are not interchangeable: a registration cannot be transferred between recreational and Part 107 use, so a student who registered a personal drone recreationally must register it again before commercial work. Registrants must be at least 13 years old and a U.S. citizen or permanent resident; a guardian registers for younger owners. [3](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/register_drone)

The weight threshold is a reliable test question: drones weighing 0.55 pounds (250 grams) or less are exempt from registration only when flown exclusively recreationally. Any drone flown under Part 107 must be registered regardless of weight. Aircraft at 55 pounds or more cannot use the online system and must register under Part 47. [3](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/register_drone)

The registration number must be marked on the drone's exterior before flight, and failure to register an aircraft that requires it can bring regulatory and criminal penalties. Registration is also the hook for [Remote Identification](/regulations/remote-identification.md): the aircraft's or broadcast module's serial number is listed in the registration record. [1](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [3](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/register_drone)

Foreign-owned or foreign-operated small UAS face an additional layer, needing a Foreign Aircraft Permit under 14 CFR Part 375 before commercial operations. This rarely matters for a community college course but explains the citizenship questions on the registration form. [1](/sources/ac-107-2a.md)
