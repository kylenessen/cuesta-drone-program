# Certificate of Waiver

A **Certificate of Waiver** (CoW) is the FAA authorization, established in Subpart D of [Part 107](/regulations/part-107.md), that permits a small UAS operation to deviate from specific operating rules when the Administrator finds the proposed operation can be conducted safely under the certificate's terms. The certificate is narrow by design. It lets the holder deviate only from the rules and only under the conditions named in the certificate. [3](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-200.md)

Waivers are how commercial operators today fly beyond visual line of sight, fly without the standard anti-collision lighting required for night or civil twilight, operate over people or moving vehicles outside the category system, or place multiple aircraft under one pilot. The knowledge test treats waiver policy lightly, with a single ACS knowledge element, but professionally it is a major topic because it defines the ceiling of what a certificated remote pilot can be hired to do under Part 107. [1](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## Requirements

Section 107.200 authorizes the FAA to issue a waiver when the proposed small UAS operation can be conducted safely under the waiver's terms. A request must completely describe the operation and justify why the operation remains safe. The FAA may add limitations it considers necessary, and the recipient must comply with every condition written into the certificate. [3](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-200.md)

Section 107.205 lists the rules that can be waived. The table below translates that legal list into operational meaning for instruction. [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md)

| Rule | What the waiver covers | Teaching emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section 107.25 | Operation from a moving vehicle or aircraft. The rule cannot be waived to allow carriage of another person's property for compensation or hire. | A moving land or water vehicle operation may still need to solve VLOS, visual observer communication, vehicle safety, and local traffic-law issues. [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md) |
| Section 107.29(a)(2) and (b) | Anti-collision lighting for night and civil twilight operations. | Since the 2021 rule change, night flight itself is routine with current training and lighting. This waiver is about the lighting requirement, not general night privileges. [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [7](/topics/night-operations.md) |
| Section 107.31 | Visual line of sight. The rule cannot be waived to allow carriage of another person's property for compensation or hire. | This is the BVLOS waiver. The safety case must explain what replaces unaided visual awareness, including containment, detection, procedures, personnel, and failure handling. [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md) [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md) |
| Section 107.33 | Visual observer requirements. | A visual observer waiver does not erase the remote PIC's responsibility for awareness. It changes how the applicant proposes to satisfy that responsibility. [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md) [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md) |
| Section 107.35 | Operation of multiple small unmanned aircraft systems. | This is the route for one remote PIC to operate more than one aircraft. The safety case needs command, deconfliction, automation, lost-link, and emergency plans. [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md) [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md) |
| Section 107.37(a) | Yielding the right of way. | This is a high-burden waiver because the standard rule protects other aircraft. The applicant must show how other aircraft remain protected even when the normal yielding rule is modified. [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md) |
| Section 107.39 | Operation over people. | A waiver remains the path when an operation cannot fit the Part 107 Subpart E categories for [Operations Over People](/regulations/operations-over-people.md). [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md) [8](/regulations/operations-over-people.md) |
| Section 107.41 | Operation in certain airspace. | Controlled airspace can require both an operational waiver and an [Airspace Authorization](/topics/airspace-authorization.md). Current FAA instructions say a waivered operation in controlled airspace needs the operational waiver in Aviation Safety Hub and the airspace authorization in FAADroneZone. [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md) [6](/sources/ashub-waiver-instructions.md) |
| Section 107.51 | Operating limits for groundspeed, altitude, visibility, and cloud clearance. | Aviation Safety Hub treats these as separate 107.51(a) through 107.51(d) waiver items. A request must show why relaxing one limit does not undermine containment, see-and-avoid, or weather margins. [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md) [6](/sources/ashub-waiver-instructions.md) |
| Section 107.145 | Operations over moving vehicles. | A waiver remains the path when the operation cannot fit the moving-vehicle limits built into the Part 107 category system. [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md) [8](/regulations/operations-over-people.md) |

Some nonwaivable limits are as important as the waivable list. The 55-pound small UAS limit is not listed in section 107.205, and section 107.25 and section 107.31 both preserve the compensation-based property-carriage restriction. A waiver also does not replace aircraft registration, remote pilot certification, Remote ID compliance, or controlled-airspace authorization when those requirements separately apply. [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [3](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-200.md) [4](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-205.md)

## Application Process

Current FAA guidance says the Part 107 operational waiver application process has transitioned to Aviation Safety Hub. Previously submitted waiver applications continue to be processed in FAADroneZone, while Part 107 airspace authorizations remain in FAADroneZone. This matters for teaching because older FAA guidance and older course notes can still point students toward DroneZone for operational waivers. [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md) [6](/sources/ashub-waiver-instructions.md)

Aviation Safety Hub asks for operator information, the responsible party, the proposed operation, UAS devices, operating dates, location, waiver associations, requested waivers, supporting documentation, and safety evaluation responses. The proposed start date cannot be in the past or more than 48 months in the future, and the FAA instruction recommends choosing a start date at least 90 days from submission. If the waivered operation will occur in controlled airspace, the applicant must submit the operational waiver in Aviation Safety Hub and the airspace authorization in FAADroneZone. [6](/sources/ashub-waiver-instructions.md)

The FAA's current public page says it will try to review and approve or disapprove waiver requests within 90 days of submission, with timing affected by complexity and by the completeness of the initial application. If the FAA needs more information, it sends the request through Aviation Safety Hub to the responsible person listed on the application. If the applicant does not respond within 30 days, the application is canceled and must be resubmitted. [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md)

This updates the older AC 107-2A planning language that encouraged filing at least 60 days before the proposed operation. The AC remains useful for Part 107 interpretation, but current curriculum should teach the 90-day Aviation Safety Hub expectation for operational waiver planning. [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md) [6](/sources/ashub-waiver-instructions.md)

## Safety Case

A waiver application is a safety case, not just a request for permission. FAA guidance tells applicants to describe the proposed operation, identify operational risks, explain mitigation methods, and address each Waiver Safety Explanation Guideline in detail. The FAA says an application that does not identify hazards and mitigation strategies cannot support a complete safety analysis and may be disapproved for insufficient information. [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md)

The common safety-case categories are concrete. The applicant should define where the operation will occur, how high the aircraft will fly, whether controlled airspace is involved, what other airspace exists within 5 miles, and what kind of area will be overflown. The applicant should also describe the UAS type, power source, flight time, range, speed, physical size, containment method, termination system, weight, payload, and any object that could drop or detach. [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md)

Personnel and training also matter. The FAA asks what minimum experience the remote PIC will have, how many people are needed, whether visual observers or other personnel will be used, how they will be trained, how competence will be evaluated, and whether recurrent training or testing will be required. This makes waivers a useful classroom bridge between regulation and operational risk management. [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md)

The FAA's review focus is unusual circumstances. A strong application explains how the operation stays safe when the ordinary Part 107 rule no longer applies, what failure conditions can arise, and how procedures, technology, equipment, people, operating limitations, or restricted access areas reduce those risks. The FAA also tells applicants to include only information that applies to the waiver sections requested, which is a useful discipline for students writing mock safety cases. [5](/sources/faa-part-107-waivers.md)

One nuance worth teaching is that waivers are rule-specific. A BVLOS waiver does not automatically waive altitude, cloud clearance, or controlled-airspace requirements. An operation over people at night can require the over-people framework, the night lighting rule, and the waiver terms to be satisfied at the same time. [3](/sources/govinfo-14-cfr-107-200.md) [7](/topics/night-operations.md) [8](/regulations/operations-over-people.md)

The waiver era for beyond visual line of sight flight may be changing. The pending [Part 108 BVLOS Rulemaking](/regulations/part-108-bvlos-rulemaking.md) proposes replacing many per-operation BVLOS waivers with a standing regulatory framework, but Part 107 section 107.31 remains the current law until a final rule changes it. [9](/regulations/part-108-bvlos-rulemaking.md)
