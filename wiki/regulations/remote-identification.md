# Remote Identification

**Remote Identification** (Remote ID) is the requirement, codified in 14 CFR Part 89, that a drone in flight broadcast identification and location information receivable by other parties, functioning as a digital license plate. It lets the FAA and law enforcement locate the control station of a drone flying unsafely or where it is not allowed. Since September 16, 2023, most drones that are registered or required to be registered must comply, whether flown under [Part 107](/regulations/part-107.md), recreationally, or by public safety agencies. The 2021 ACS revision added a Remote ID task, so the compliance paths and broadcast rules are tested material. [1](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [3](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/remote_id)

## Requirements

There are three ways to comply. A standard Remote ID aircraft broadcasts from built-in equipment, and its serial number is entered in the aircraft's registration record. A broadcast module is a retrofit device attached to an older aircraft; it broadcasts the module's serial number and the takeoff location, and an aircraft flying on a broadcast module must remain within visual line of sight. Finally, an aircraft with no Remote ID equipment may fly only inside an FAA-Recognized Identification Area (FRIA), a defined geographic area where broadcast is not required, again within visual line of sight. [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md) [3](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/remote_id)

The broadcast includes the identity of the aircraft (serial number or session ID), the location and altitude of both the aircraft and its control station (or takeoff point for modules), velocity, a time mark, and emergency status, which the ACS tests as the minimum message elements. Small unmanned aircraft are separately prohibited from using ATC transponders or ADS-B Out to satisfy identification, absent specific authorization. [1](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) [3](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/remote_id)

FRIAs matter directly to the Cuesta program: community-based organizations and educational institutions may apply for them, and the SLO Flyers club field adjacent to the SLO campus, discussed in course planning as a flight training site, is designated as a FRIA. That designation allows student flying with aircraft that lack Remote ID equipment. The FRIA status claim should be reverified against FAA data before being repeated in official documents; it currently rests on the program's own planning notes. [4](/sources/aero-100-course-outline.md)

Remote ID also gates some operations: sustained flight over open-air assemblies under [Operations Over People](/regulations/operations-over-people.md) requires standard Remote ID compliance. [2](/sources/ac-107-2a.md)
