# Remote Pilot Certificate

The **Remote Pilot Certificate** with a small UAS rating is the FAA airman certificate required to act as remote pilot in command under [Part 107](/regulations/part-107.md). It is the credential AERO 100 students are working toward, colloquially called a drone license, and it carries both initial requirements and an ongoing knowledge recency obligation that students should understand before they invest in the test.

## Requirements

To be eligible, an applicant must be at least 16 years old, able to read, speak, write, and understand English (with allowance for medical exceptions such as hearing impairment), and in a physical and mental condition that allows safe operation of a small UAS. There is no aviation medical certificate requirement; fitness is self-assessed against the standard in the rule. [1](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot) [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

The standard path runs through the [Part 107 Knowledge Test](/exams/part-107-knowledge-test.md): obtain an FAA Tracking Number through IACRA, pass the UAG test at a PSI testing center, apply on FAA Form 8710-13 through IACRA, and clear TSA security vetting before the temporary certificate is issued. Holders of a Part 61 pilot certificate with a flight review current within 24 calendar months may instead complete the free online course ALC-451 and validate their identity with a CFI, an Airman Certification Representative, a FSDO, or a designated examiner. [1](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot) [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

Certificate holders must have the certificate accessible during operations and present it on request to the FAA, NTSB, TSA, or law enforcement. Offenses involving alcohol or drugs, or refusal to submit to testing, are grounds for denial of an application or suspension or revocation of the certificate. [2](/sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) [3](/sources/ac-107-2a.md)

## Keeping It Current

The certificate itself does not expire, but the privilege to exercise it does: the holder must have completed either the initial knowledge test or recurrent training within the previous 24 calendar months. Since 2021 the recurrent requirement is met with free FAA online training (course ALC-677 for Part 107-only pilots), which also delivers the night operations training that unlocked routine [Night Operations](/topics/night-operations.md). A calendar-month subtlety worth teaching: 24 calendar months means through the end of the month, so a test passed on 2026-03-10 keeps a pilot current through 2028-03-31. [1](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot) [3](/sources/ac-107-2a.md)
