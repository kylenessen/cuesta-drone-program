Proposal Rationale

This course is designed to meet the need for remote unmanned aerial vehicle operators in the district.  The FAA 

requires pilots utilizing small unmanned aerial vehicles to have an FAA Airman Certificate pursuant 14 CFR Part 
107.  Unmanned Aerial Vehicle technology is a growing industry as San Luis Obispo County is emerging as a 

leader in the design and manufacture of these aircraft.  As this technology gains acceptance, there is anticipation 
of increased usage in existing college programs to include survey/ civil engineering, real-estate and construction, 

criminal justice, agriculture, transportation and logistics, fire technology, as well as aviation maintenance 

programs.  This course is a foundational course as part of a broader integration of remote applications
Catalog Description

Prepares students to meet the Federal Aviation Administration requirements for certification to operate 
Unmanned Aircraft Vehicles under 55 pounds for commercial operations pursuant  to 14 CFR 107.  Emphasizes 

the safe, legal, and ethical aspects of UAV operations in civil, public, and commercial settings.  Includes special 

emphasis on capabilities and limitations of optical payloads and sensors, flight operation planning, and safety.  
Prepares students to safely operate UAV through all phases of flight to National Institute of Standards and 

Technology Level 2 Open Area Maneuvering remote pilot proficiency standards.

Assignments

All Fields

Critical Thinking Assignments (minimum 2 examples):
Perform preflight calculations to determine if flight can be safely conducted based on aircraft

performance, knowledge of aviation weather, forecast products, and airspace.

(Obj 2, 3, 4)

Independent Work (minimum 2 examples):

Perform small unmanned aerial vehicle flight to the standard test methods found in NIST sUAS open
lane Level II standards by maneuvering a sUAS over selected targets in specific sequence and land

aircraft safely.
(Obj 5)

Critical Thinking Assignments (minimum 2 examples):

Based on knowledge of the weather, approved aviation weather forecast sources, and mission
requirements, determine if flight can be safely conducted near an airport with an operating control

tower.

(Obj 4)

Independent Work (minimum 2 examples):

Perform a technical preflight inspection of aircraft in accordance with the manufacturer's
recommendations, determine if safe for flight, and calculate expected in-flight endurance.

(Obj 1, 2, 3)

Course Description
Prepares students to meet the Federal Aviation Administration requirements for certification to operate

Unmanned Aircraft Vehicles under 55 pounds for commercial operations pursuant to 14 CFR 107. Emphasizes
the safe, legal, and ethical aspects of UAV operations in civil, public, and commercial settings. Includes special

emphasis on capabilities and limitations of optical payloads and sensors, flight operation planning, and

safety. Prepares students to safely operate UAV through all phases of flight to National Institute of Standards
and Technology Level 2 Open Area Maneuvering remote pilot proficiency standards.

Objectives

All Fields

1.  Perform a flight of an unmanned aerial vehicle to the "Open Lane Level II NIST Standard Test Methods
for small Unmanned Aircraft Systems (sUAS). The U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology

(NIST) quantitatively evaluates various system capabilities and remote pilot proficiency.

2.  Demonstrate knowledge of regulations, airport operations procedures, aviation weather, radio

communication procedures, emergency procedures, airspace, and navigation to FAA Part 107 standards.

3.  Implement appropriate decision making and judgment to mitigate risk based on human factor

considerations.

4.  Calculate the performance of UAV to include weight and balance, takeoff and climb, endurance, and

aerodynamic effects based on actual and forecast aviation weather.

5.  Perform preflight inspection to identify maintenance deficiencies that adversely affect aircraft

performance.
