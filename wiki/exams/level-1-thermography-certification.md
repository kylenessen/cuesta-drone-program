# Level 1 Thermography Certification

**Level 1 Thermography Certification** is the credential that turns a drone pilot who owns a thermal camera into an inspector whose findings clients will accept. For utility, solar, and building inspection work it functions the way the [Part 107 Knowledge Test](part-107-knowledge-test.md) functions for flying at all. No government body issues it. Certification comes from training organizations, chiefly the Infrared Training Center (ITC, associated with Teledyne FLIR) and the Infraspection Institute, following personnel qualification guidelines published by the American Society for Nondestructive Testing (ASNT). For Cuesta's inspection course the certification matters twice over, once as the employment credential graduates will pursue and once as the de facto syllabus for what a thermography unit must teach.

## Format

Thermography is treated as a nondestructive testing (NDT) method, and NDT personnel qualification in the United States runs on an employer-based model. ASNT's recommended practice SNT-TC-1A gives employers a framework for qualifying and certifying their own technicians, with written practices, documented training hours, and examinations signed off by a Level 3 [1]. Training providers deliver courses whose learning objectives, contact hours, and written exams align with SNT-TC-1A and the companion standard ANSI/ASNT CP-105, and they issue certificates on that basis [2] [3].

The scheme has three levels. A Level 1 thermographer collects data and performs inspections following established procedures. A Level 2 thermographer sets up equipment, interprets results, and supervises Level 1 work. A Level 3 thermographer writes procedures, establishes techniques, and trains and examines the levels below [2]. For a drone operator entering inspection work, Level 1 is the relevant credential, and Level 2 becomes valuable for those who move into report review and program management.

In practice the pathway is a 32 hour course ending in a written certification exam. ITC runs it as four days of eight hour sessions, delivered in classroom, live online, and on-site formats, with an electronically administered exam at the end. There are no formal prerequisites, though students need access to an infrared camera and a laptop or tablet for the exam [3]. ITC also offers a drone-specific form, Level 1 sUAS Thermography, a four day course that adds sUAS safety, camera and lens selection for aerial work, and aerial application technique to the same CP-105 aligned learning objectives and exam [4]. The Infraspection Institute runs its Level 1 as a four day open enrollment class or a 32 hour distance learning program, including a drone-specific sUAS distance course, with a 66 question exam and an 80 percent passing score [5] [6]. ABJ Drone Academy sells a lower cost online drone thermography course, best understood as an inexpensive introduction rather than a substitute where a client specifies an SNT-TC-1A aligned credential [7]. ASNT itself offers examinations in the thermal/infrared method, including the NDT Level 3 national exam, a route that matters in industrial NDT careers more than in drone services [1] [2].

## Content

Because certification exams follow the published SNT-TC-1A and CP-105 learning objectives, the Level 1 body of knowledge is effectively a syllabus. Provider outlines for the 32 hour course cover thermal physics and temperature scales, the three modes of heat transfer, the electromagnetic spectrum and radiation laws, blackbodies and emissivity, detector and camera operation, image formation including field of view and atmospheric attenuation, qualitative versus quantitative technique, the major application families in electrical, mechanical, building envelope, and roof inspection, and report writing [8].

That subject matter is developed across this wiki as teachable units. The sensor and physics layer is [Thermal Imaging](../topics/thermal-imaging.md), resting on the [Electromagnetic Spectrum](../topics/electromagnetic-spectrum.md). The measurement science is [Emissivity and Reflected Temperature](../topics/emissivity-and-reflected-temperature.md) and [Spot Size and Measurement Distance](../topics/spot-size-and-measurement-distance.md). The craft layer is [Thermal Tuning and Palettes](../topics/thermal-tuning-and-palettes.md), and the interpretation discipline is [Delta-T Severity Classification](../topics/delta-t-severity-classification.md). A college course cannot issue the certification, but it can deliver this knowledge so students walk into a four day certification course, or an exam, already fluent.

## Logistics

ITC registration runs about $2,200 through authorized resellers, with list prices somewhat higher depending on provider and format [9]. ITC certification is valid for five years, and renewal requires a recertification exam or refresher course along with continuing education or professional contributions and continued active work in thermography [10]. Infraspection certification does not expire and carries no renewal fees [6], and its distance learning format prices separately from the exam fee. ABJ's online course is typically advertised in the hundreds of dollars rather than thousands [7]. Zeitview, one of the largest drone inspection marketplaces, points its pilots to the ITC sUAS course as the route into thermal roof, solar, and power line work [11].

## Employer Expectations and Career Value

Whether Level 1 is formally required varies by client, but the market treats it as the professional floor. Industry guidance for drone pilots reports that government agencies, insurers, and industrial clients follow strict thermal inspection guidelines and often will not accept reports from uncertified operators [12]. Job boards carry standing listings for thermography technicians in utility and solar inspection that name Level 1 certification as a requirement [13], and California utility inspection programs sit inside formal condition-rating regimes where defensible severity classification matters, as described in [Infrastructure Inspection Operations](../topics/infrastructure-inspection-operations.md). The safe curriculum assumption is that a graduate who wants paid thermal inspection work will need Level 1, and that the credential distinguishes a candidate even where it is not mandated.

The pay evidence, with the caveat that much of it comes from training providers and job boards, points one direction. ZipRecruiter listed the average for drone thermography work in the United States at roughly $117,000 per year in April 2026, with most positions between $94,500 and $140,000 [14]. Career guidance for drone pilots reports that thermal inspection jobs routinely pay 30 to 50 percent more than comparable visual-only inspection work, with energy and utility inspection the highest paying drone specialty [12]. Against a course cost near $2,200, the certification is one of the cheapest credential investments in the drone industry relative to its earning effect, which is why it anchors the career case for an inspection course.
