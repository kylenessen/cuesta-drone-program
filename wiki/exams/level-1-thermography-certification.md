# Thermography Certification

**Thermography Certification** is the credential pathway that turns a drone pilot who owns a thermal camera into an inspector whose findings clients will accept. The core credential is Level 1 thermographer certification, and for utility, solar, and building inspection work it functions the way Part 107 functions for flying at all. No government body issues it. Certification comes from training organizations, chiefly the Infrared Training Center (ITC, associated with Teledyne FLIR) and the Infraspection Institute, following personnel qualification guidelines published by the American Society for Nondestructive Testing (ASNT). For Cuesta's inspection course the certification matters twice over, once as the employment credential graduates will pursue and once as the de facto syllabus for what a thermography unit must teach.

## How thermographer certification works

Thermography is treated as a nondestructive testing (NDT) method, and NDT personnel qualification in the United States runs on an employer-based model. ASNT's recommended practice SNT-TC-1A gives employers a framework for qualifying and certifying their own technicians, with written practices, documented training hours, and examinations signed off by a Level III [1]. Training providers such as ITC and Infraspection deliver courses whose learning objectives, contact hours, and written exams align with SNT-TC-1A and the companion standard ANSI/ASNT CP-105, and they issue certificates on that basis [2] [3].

The scheme has three levels. A Level 1 thermographer collects data and performs inspections following established procedures. A Level 2 thermographer sets up equipment, interprets results, and supervises Level 1 work. A Level 3 thermographer writes procedures, establishes techniques, and trains and examines the levels below [2]. For a drone operator entering inspection work, Level 1 is the relevant credential, and Level 2 becomes valuable for those who move into report review and program management.

## The ITC Level 1 course

The most widely recognized pathway is ITC's Level 1 certification course. It runs four days with eight hour sessions, delivered in classroom, live online, and on-site formats, and it carries 32 hours of instruction [3]. There are no formal prerequisites, though students need access to an infrared camera and a laptop or tablet for the electronically administered certification exam at the end of the course [3]. The curriculum covers infrared science, emissivity and reflectivity, heat transfer, atmospheric effects, thermal tuning and image analysis, safety around energized equipment, report writing, and application modules for electrical, mechanical, building envelope, and roof inspection [3]. Registration runs about $2,200 through authorized resellers, with list prices somewhat higher depending on provider and format [4].

ITC also offers the course in a drone-specific form, Level 1 sUAS Thermography, a four day course focused on aerial infrared inspection. It adds sUAS safety, camera and lens selection for aerial work, and aerial application technique to the same CP-105 aligned learning objectives and written exam [5]. Zeitview, one of the largest drone inspection marketplaces, points its pilots to exactly this course as the route into thermal roof, solar, and power line work [6].

ITC certification is valid for five years. Renewal requires a recertification exam or refresher course along with continuing education or professional contributions and continued active work in thermography [7].

## Competing credentials

The Infraspection Institute is the main independent alternative. Its Level 1 course runs as a four day open enrollment class or a 32 hour distance learning program, including a drone-specific Level 1 sUAS Thermography distance course [8] [9]. The certification exam has 66 questions with an 80 percent passing score, and unlike ITC, Infraspection certification does not expire and carries no renewal fees [10].

ABJ Drone Academy sells a lower cost online drone thermography Level 1 course, typically advertised in the hundreds of dollars rather than thousands, covering infrared science fundamentals and thermal image interpretation for pilots with no prior thermography background [11]. It is best understood as an inexpensive introduction rather than a substitute where a client specifies an SNT-TC-1A aligned credential.

ASNT itself publishes the qualification documents and offers its own examinations in the thermal/infrared testing method, including the ASNT NDT Level III national exam. That route matters in industrial NDT careers more than in drone services, but it anchors the whole system, and course providers advertise their alignment with it [1] [2].

## Employer and client expectations

Whether Level 1 is formally required varies by client, but the market treats it as the professional floor. Industry guidance for drone pilots reports that government agencies, insurers, and industrial clients follow strict thermal inspection guidelines and often will not accept reports from uncertified operators [12]. Zeitview markets the certification as the gateway to industrial clients in roofing, solar, and power line work [6]. Job boards carry standing listings for thermography technicians in utility and solar inspection that name Level 1 certification as a requirement [13], and California utility inspection programs sit inside formal condition-rating regimes where defensible severity classification matters, as described in [Infrastructure Inspection Operations](/topics/infrastructure-inspection-operations.md). The safe curriculum assumption is that a graduate who wants paid thermal inspection work will need Level 1, and that the credential distinguishes a candidate even where it is not mandated.

## Career value

The pay evidence, with the caveat that much of it comes from training providers and job boards, points one direction. ZipRecruiter listed the average for drone thermography work in the United States at roughly $117,000 per year in April 2026, with most positions between $94,500 and $140,000 [14]. Career guidance for drone pilots reports that thermal inspection jobs routinely pay 30 to 50 percent more than comparable visual-only inspection work, with energy and utility inspection the highest paying drone specialty [12]. Against a course cost near $2,200, the certification is one of the cheapest credential investments in the drone industry relative to its earning effect, which is why it anchors the career case for an inspection course.

## What a preparation course must teach

Because certification exams follow the SNT-TC-1A and CP-105 learning objectives, the Level 1 body of knowledge is effectively a syllabus. A course preparing students for it must teach infrared physics and heat transfer, emissivity, reflectivity, and apparent versus true temperature, thermal tuning and palettes, spot size and measurement distance, qualitative versus quantitative technique, environmental factors such as solar loading, wind, and load, and delta-T severity classification, plus the drone-specific complications of radiometric payloads and flying height. All of that subject matter is developed in [Thermal Imaging](/topics/thermal-imaging.md), and the physics foundation sits in [Electromagnetic Spectrum](/topics/electromagnetic-spectrum.md). A college course cannot issue the certification, but it can deliver the knowledge so students walk into a four day certification course, or an exam, already fluent.
