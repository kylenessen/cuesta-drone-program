# Part 107 Knowledge Test

The **Part 107 Knowledge Test**, formally the Unmanned Aircraft General, Small (UAG) certification knowledge test, is the FAA exam a person must pass to earn a [Remote Pilot Certificate](../regulations/remote-pilot-certificate.md) with a small UAS rating. It is a proctored, computer-based, multiple-choice test administered at PSI testing centers. Passing it is the single gate between a new student and commercial drone privileges, which makes it the organizing target for AERO 100's aeronautical knowledge content.

The test measures the knowledge areas listed in 14 CFR parts 89 and 107, as codified in the Airman Certification Standards, [FAA-S-ACS-10B](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md). Course coverage can be audited task by task against that document.

## Format

The test consists of 60 objective multiple-choice questions, each with a single correct response and each independent of the others. The applicant has 2 hours, and a score of 70 percent or better (42 of 60 questions) passes. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

Questions are distributed across five content areas [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md):

| Area | Share of test |
| --- | --- |
| I. Regulations | 15 to 25 percent |
| II. Airspace and Requirements | 15 to 25 percent |
| III. Weather | 11 to 16 percent |
| IV. Loading and Performance | 7 to 11 percent |
| V. Operations | 35 to 45 percent |

Operations and Regulations together carry roughly half to two thirds of the test. Many questions reference figures in the FAA Airman Knowledge Testing Supplement (FAA-CT-8080-2), a booklet of sectional chart excerpts and airport diagrams provided by the proctor, so chart reading is a tested skill rather than background knowledge. The FAA publishes representative [sample questions](../sources/uag-sample-questions.md) showing the style. [2](../sources/uag-sample-questions.md)

## Content

The tested content is defined by five Areas of Operation in the ACS, each divided into Tasks with coded knowledge elements (for example, UA.I.B.K10 is visual line of sight operations under Regulations, Operating Rules). The areas map to this wiki as follows. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

Area I, Regulations, covers [Part 107](../regulations/part-107.md) generally, the [Part 107 Operating Rules](../regulations/part-107-operating-rules.md), certification requirements, [Certificate of Waiver](../regulations/certificate-of-waiver.md) policy, [Operations Over People](../regulations/operations-over-people.md), and [Remote Identification](../regulations/remote-identification.md). Area II covers [Airspace Classification](../topics/airspace-classification.md) and [Airspace Authorization](../topics/airspace-authorization.md) requirements. Area III covers [Aviation Weather Sources](../topics/aviation-weather-sources.md) and [Weather Effects on Performance](../topics/weather-effects-on-performance.md). Area IV covers [Loading and Performance](../topics/loading-and-performance.md). Area V, the largest, covers [Radio Communications](../topics/radio-communications.md), [Airport Operations](../topics/airport-operations.md), [Emergency Procedures](../topics/emergency-procedures.md), [Aeronautical Decision-Making](../topics/aeronautical-decision-making.md), [Remote Pilot Physiology](../topics/remote-pilot-physiology.md), and [Maintenance and Inspection](../topics/maintenance-and-inspection.md).

When an applicant misses a question, the resulting Airman Knowledge Test Report lists the ACS code of the deficient element, which is how retest preparation and instruction can be targeted. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

## Logistics

To register, an applicant first creates an IACRA profile to obtain an FAA Tracking Number (FTN), then schedules with the FAA's testing vendor, PSI Services, at one of its testing centers. Applicants must be at least 14 years old to take the test (the certificate itself requires age 16) and must present photo identification showing signature and date of birth. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md) [3](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot)

The proctor provides the testing supplement booklet, and simple calculators and plotting tools are permitted within the limits listed in the ACS appendices. An applicant who fails must wait 14 calendar days to retest, but needs no instructor endorsement. A passing Airman Knowledge Test Report remains valid for applying for the certificate for 24 calendar months. [1](../sources/faa-s-acs-10b.md)

After passing, the applicant applies for the certificate through IACRA on FAA Form 8710-13, undergoes TSA security vetting, receives a temporary certificate electronically, and later receives the permanent card by mail. Holders of a Part 61 pilot certificate with a current flight review may skip the UAG test entirely and instead complete the free FAA online course ALC-451. [3](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot)

The knowledge does not stay current on its own: certificate holders must complete free online recurrent training every 24 calendar months to keep operating privileges, as described under [Remote Pilot Certificate](../regulations/remote-pilot-certificate.md). [3](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot)
