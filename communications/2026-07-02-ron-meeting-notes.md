## Overview

A productive meeting focused on charging through the bureaucratic process to get the drone program approved, with a launch target of October. ## Course Structure

We landed on a new structure that I like better than the earlier hub and spoke idea: a trunk. Students take an intro class (AERO 100), then an advanced drone applications class (AERO 110), and from there we can add one unit booster classes if we want them. This feels much more achievable. Each class becomes more flexible, and with more content to choose from in a semester, the instructor is never thin on material. That is the stronger position to be in.

Ron is comfortable with me developing the classes where I have the most experience first, focusing on AERO 100 and AERO 110 (advanced drone mapping). He will work the agricultural angle on the side; pushing in that direction is important to Aubrey, and Part 137 (ag operators) is the relevant regulation there. Part 108 also came up as something industry partners consider important, and I need to look into it.

## Approval Pathway

The program needs formal approval, and the sequence runs: college council has given initial approval, planning and budget is next, then academic senate, then back to college council for final approval, after which the curriculum committee can approve. The program needs 16 units to qualify for financial aid. I still need to find out who sits on college council, academic senate, and planning and budget.

Key dates that fell out of the meeting:

- **By July 31:** course content outline well established, with feedback from advisory members, broken out into domain specifics.
- **Mid August:** academic senate. Before that, a large formal meeting with the advisory committee with votes to approve the course content.
- **By end of September:** CurriculumNet submission, which involves calculating course hours, class descriptions, student learning outcomes, requisites, and consideration of hybrid options, following the pattern of AERO 100.

AERO 100 itself could run next spring or summer, depending on enthusiasm and progress on program approval.

## Content Development Approach

Course content will be developed in this repo as a knowledge base: a wiki style knowledge graph of the topics I want to teach, with tags marking which courses each topic applies to so nothing gets duplicated. Student learning outcomes live in the wiki, associated with tags and linked to content. Course outline pages link directly to the topics they cover and could be built programmatically. This matches the design principle established earlier: content comes first, teaching materials attach to it, and lectures and labs are pulled from those. Build it once.

One open problem is collaboration with reviewers who are not versed in GitHub or Markdown. A static page with expandable topics and email replies would be acceptable, but I would prefer inline comments that other reviewers can see. Overleaf did not work well for this. Open Knowledge deserves a second look.
