# Program Strategy Discussion with Ron
**Date:** January 10, 2026
**Participants:** Ron (Program Director for Aerospace Classes)

## Overview

Today's discussion with Ron focused on structuring the drone program curriculum at Cuesta College and defining a clear path toward program success. The conversation clarified both immediate priorities and longer-term possibilities, including a potential path to full-time employment if the program achieves its goals.

## Course Structure

We settled on a focused curriculum built around three core courses, each designed to meet specific student needs while contributing to measurable program outcomes.

The foundational **100-level course** serves dual purposes: preparing students for FAA Part 107 certification and building practical flight competency. This course will expose students to diverse drone applications, helping them discover areas of interest and motivating continued engagement with the program. The variety of applications ensures students can find pathways that resonate with their career goals.

The **photogrammetry class** centers on producing color orthophotos. This practical focus gives students marketable skills in a growing field. My next task is to formally develop the course description and complete the necessary documentation to move this forward.

We also discussed a **safety course**, which I have less direct experience with but believe I can develop effectively through research and collaboration. This course fills a critical gap in ensuring students understand operational safety requirements.

## Strategic Decisions

Ron discouraged adding an advanced mapping class to the initial program structure, and his reasoning was sound. Program success at Cuesta is measured by certification completion rates. We need students to finish the program, and introducing courses that may not run regularly creates barriers to completion. If a course becomes part of the program requirements but doesn't consistently fill, we effectively sabotage our own success metrics.

This approach also aligns with our existing resources. We already have the equipment infrastructure necessary for the planned courses, including a quality mapping drone. We'll supplement this with smaller, less expensive drones for the 100-level course, making flight training accessible while protecting our primary assets.

## Future Considerations

An **agricultural drone class** combining spraying and mapping techniques represents a compelling phase two development. This advanced course would leverage both skill sets and address real industry needs. I believe I could develop and teach this class effectively, but we both agreed it should wait until the core program is established and running smoothly. Ron is feeling stretched with current responsibilities, and I haven't yet had the opportunity to complete all the mapping projects on my plate.

## Career Development

There appears to be a clear path toward full-time employment if this program succeeds in meeting its certification and completion goals. This makes investing time and effort into thoughtful program development particularly worthwhile.
