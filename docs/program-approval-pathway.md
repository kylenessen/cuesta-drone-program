# Program Approval Pathway

This page maps the governance route the drone program must travel to formal approval, along with who sits on each body. The target is a program launch by October 2026. The sequence below comes from Ron, and the membership rosters come from Cuesta's published governance documents as of July 2026.

## The Sequence

1. **College Council** has given initial approval.
2. **Planning and Budget Committee** is the next stop.
3. **Academic Senate** reviews in mid August. The advisory committee must have met and voted on the course content before this step.
4. **College Council** gives final approval.
5. **Curriculum Committee** approves the courses. Course submissions go through CurrIQunet, which is the system's actual name. Repo documents that say CurriculumNet mean this system. Faculty accounts are managed by the curriculum coordinator.

## The 16 Unit Constraint

The program needs at least 16 units for students to qualify for federal financial aid. This is the federal Title IV floor for undergraduate certificate programs, which must be at least 16 semester credit hours. This constraint shapes how many courses the program designs toward and how units are distributed across them.

## Observations

Several names repeat across bodies. Erich Tucker sits on Planning and Budget and is Academic Senate President. Greg Baxley and Alex Kahane appear on both central committees. Jason Curtis, as VP of Instruction, co-chairs College Council and sits on Planning and Budget. Winning over a small number of people carries weight across multiple steps of the pathway.

The drone program sits in Skilled Trades and Technology territory, so Jonathan Blackketter is the natural Senate representative to brief before the mid August meeting. Matt Knudsen chairs the Curriculum Committee, the final gate, and also sits on the Senate, so an early conversation there could smooth the CurrIQunet submission planned for the end of September.
