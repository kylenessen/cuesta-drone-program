# Planning Drone Program Purchases and Curriculum

This is a planning document for purchasing equipment and developing curriculum for two different drone classes. The goal is to create a spreadsheet of options that is understandable and allows for experimentation.

## Initial Drone Purchases

We know we are getting an A4E and an A4T. This covers the public sector work across 14 countries. Ron seems to know more about this type of work, so that's a good start.

## Mapping Course Breakdown

This section outlines the equipment, software, and curriculum for the advanced mapping and photogrammetry course.

### RTK and Accuracy

I definitely want a base station. We are paying for the ability to have RTK, and it's essential for support and accuracy. If I were doing this for myself, I would spend the money on RTK every time.

**Decision Point: Emlid vs. Trimble**

* **Option 1: Teach what I know and believe is correct (Emlid).**
  * **Pros:** Emlid is a great value. If a student is starting a drone program from zero, Emlid is the better choice.
  * **Cons:** It may not be the industry standard in all organizations.
* **Option 2: Teach the industry standard (Trimble).**
  * **Pros:** Many companies already have Trimble gear, so students would be learning on familiar equipment.
  * **Cons:** Trimble is not a good value.

**Conclusion:** I think we should get the Emlid units. A good drone pilot in this day and age needs to be adaptable to different tools. Even if a company has Trimble gear, the skills should be transferable.

**Action Item:** Verify that the Emlid units work really well with the two mapping drones we're buying.

### Hardware and Accessories

* **Emlid Units:** A pair of Emlid units.
* **Ground Support:** A tripod for the base station and a decent pole with a bipod.
* **Ground Control Points:** Equipment for ground markers. This is a fundamental part of the curriculum and a reasonable ask.
* **Batteries:** Extra batteries are a must. We need to determine how many.
* **Field Charging:** We need options for field charging. This could be a DJI charger unit or a solar generator like my EcoFlow Delta Pro. A good field charger also serves as a good office charger, which is compelling.

### Software

**1. Flight Planning Software**

This is a critical component, especially with terrain following being essential in our area.

* **UGCs:** My preferred choice if money wasn't an issue. It's the most professional option.
* **DroneDeploy:** A strong contender. We used it for a long time. It solves two problems at once: flight planning and photogrammetry processing. It would allow us to use any computer and have a single subscription. This would get students from zero to one effectively.

**2. Photogrammetry Software**

This is a key decision, balancing ease of use, power, and cost.

* **Metashape:** This is the software I know best and believe is some of the best out there. You can grow into it, and you can own the license, which is a huge plus.
* **Pix4D:** It's subscription-based, but it's probably very good and easier to use. If they offer a good educational deal, it could be a viable option. I could always demonstrate Metashape as an alternative in my classes.
* **Open Drone Map:** It's been years since I've tried it. While I am much more technical now and the project has likely evolved, it's worth throwing in as an option.

**Decision:** The combination of **DroneDeploy** for flight planning and processing seems like the most practical ticket to get students from zero to one. I can supplement this by introducing **Metashape** for students who want to dive deeper into the intricacies of photogrammetry.

**3. Data Storage and Workflow**

Every organization will have its own workflow. We can teach a minimum viable folder structure on a hard drive. DroneDB is something I've liked in the past, but it feels more optional.

### Curriculum Topics

* **Battery Maintenance and Safety:** This could be a whole lecture. It's crucial to cover the hazards of LiPo batteries, best practices for charging (e.g., on tile), having the correct fire extinguisher, and looking for signs of damage. I should show videos of LiPo fires to emphasize the danger.
* **LiPo Battery Properties:** It's important for students to understand the non-uniform charging rate of LiPos (30-80% is much faster than 80-100%) and how that knowledge is helpful in the field.
* **Thermal Management:** All hardware needs thermal management. Equipment can heat up fast in direct sun, which can cause it to seize up. Batteries also don't like to be charged when they are very hot.
* **Remote ID:** It's worth mentioning, perhaps as a "back in my day" topic, as most new DJI drones have it integrated. It's possible students will encounter a drone that doesn't have it by default.

## Basic Drone Flying Course Breakdown

This course is focused on getting students comfortable on the sticks.

### Hardware and Accessories

* **Drones:** A fleet of DJI consumer drones. The Mini or Air line would be perfect. They are less intimidating, and it's not as catastrophic if one is lost. It's also easier to buy multiples.
  * It might be worth upgrading to the Air series for better stability in wind and longer battery life.
* **Controllers:** We absolutely need the controllers with the built-in screen. Using a separate phone is a mess.
* **Storage:** A custom Pelican case to hold all the drones, controllers, and batteries in one clean, organized box.
* **Charging:** An EcoFlow battery pack or similar solar generator. If each drone has the Fly More Combo (3 batteries), that, combined with a field charging solution, should be sufficient.
* **Accessories:**
  * A nice label maker to name and organize each drone.
  * Cheap landing pads for a clean takeoff and landing surface.

### Curriculum and Setup

* **Setup:** Each drone should have a name. We'll need to figure out the best way to manage DJI accounts—either one master account or individual email aliases for each drone.
* **Exercises:** We will develop a series of exercises to build stick skills. The goal is to have a solid kit that can be pulled out of the box, turned on, and flown immediately. We can have exercises like drawing the most perfect circle.
* **Course Materials:** Budget around $500 for materials to build out a practice course, using things like buckets or other targets. I have an idea to set up a course where I can program different lights to turn on, and students have to fly to them on a dime.
